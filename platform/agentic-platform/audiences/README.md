# Audience documents — the map

## Status
- Owner: the platform owner
- Last reviewed: 2026-09-18
- Who it is for: whoever hands one of these documents to its audience, and any reader deciding which one to read first. Each document is written for an expert in their own field who is not expected to know Google Cloud.
- What it asks of them: pick the document for the audience in front of you, hand it over in the order given below, and for any fact go to the page that defines it — the documents summarise and link; they define nothing.
- Nothing described by any of these documents is built on 2026-09-18 ([../README.md#status](../README.md#status)).

## What this set is

The platform is a secure environment on Google Cloud and Google Workspace for hundreds of agents (programs that use a language model to decide what to do and may be given tools to act), of which three are the first tenants: Wall-E the doer, Eve the independent controller, Mo the improver. The design of that platform lives in fourteen pages ([../README.md#documents](../README.md#documents)); the reasons behind it in the brief of about 200 A4 pages ([../brief/README.md](../brief/README.md)); the three ways to build it in the full build ([../setup/README.md](../setup/README.md)), the proof of value ([../pov/README.md](../pov/README.md)) and the three-day build ([../3-day/README.md](../3-day/README.md)); the status of every decision in the register of record, P1–P204 ([../12-open-decisions.md](../12-open-decisions.md)).

The documents in this folder and in [../pitch/](../pitch/) say the same things to specific audiences. Each is self-contained for its reader and none adds a fact of its own.

## How the documents relate to each other

- **One direction of facts.** The design pages define; the brief explains why and defines no fact ([../brief/32-document-map.md#precedence](../brief/32-document-map.md#precedence)); the register of record holds the *status* of every decision and wins over any page on status ([../12-open-decisions.md §1](../12-open-decisions.md#1-how-this-register-works)); these documents restate for one audience and link back. Where an audience document and a design page disagree, the design page is right and the document is corrected.
- **The plain-language set** at [../plain/README.md](../plain/README.md) is for a reader who is an expert in no relevant field. These documents assume expertise in the reader's own field and none in Google Cloud. The two sets draw on the same pages and use the same words; the glossary of record is [../brief/31-glossary.md](../brief/31-glossary.md).
- **The four expert documents** (HR and works council, security, compliance, architecture) each take one slice of the design and stop where that reader's decision stops. They overlap on purpose at the super-admin decision, because that decision touches every slice: the owner decided on 2026-09-13 that Wall-E holds Super Admin, the Workspace role with every administrative power that Google cannot limit to part of the organisation or a subset of its powers ([P33](../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md)). Every document designs around it; none re-argues it.
- **The deck and the executive brief** are one decision in two forms; the brief is what the deck asks, in one document. The sales deck and the pitch are the same pair for an audience outside the programme, and keep every rule below.
- **The build paths** are cited, never re-described: what each proves and what each can never claim is stated once, in its own README ([../setup/README.md#status](../setup/README.md#status), [../pov/README.md §1.1](../pov/README.md#11-pov_claim_statement), [../3-day/README.md §7](../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy)).

## The order a programme uses them in

Every date is an earliest date and `Assumption:`, from the build paths' own calendars ([../setup/README.md §3.4](../setup/README.md#34-parallel-sittings-and-the-critical-path), [../pov/README.md §4](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)).

1. **The works council and the DPO, before the first write — and before Eve's first stream.** On the full build's day one (planned 2026-09-21) the works-council question goes to HR and the data-protection letter to the DPO; the toil baseline waits for HR's answer ([../setup/README.md §3.4](../setup/README.md#34-parallel-sittings-and-the-critical-path)). Eve, the controller, watches every human super admin from its first run, before Wall-E exists ([../01-hld.md §13.2](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human)); that is employee monitoring and needs the DPO's record before any Eve job is deployed ([P154](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191), [P199](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204)). Worker information, or consultation where national law requires it, comes before Stage 1 of any Tier W agent acting on employee accounts ([P129](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129)); [the cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#54-employee-data-and-the-works-council), section 5.4, asks that it also precede Eve's first stream about human administrators, because that stream is workplace monitoring whatever the AI Act says about Eve; that ask is recorded there as findings X-28 and X-29, open, and is not yet in the register. `Assumption:` the jurisdiction is unknown, so whether the works council is informed or consulted is HR's answer, not a document's ([../11-tisax.md §11](../11-tisax.md#11-the-legal-and-contractual-register-p141)). The three-day build, planned for 2026-09-22 to 2026-09-24, polls the production tenant's admin audit log from its first day with no DPO record and no works-council information ([../3-day/README.md §7](../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy)); the same review asks, as finding X-03, open, that the same record — or a poll limited to its two participants with their written consent — precede it ([section 6](../14-crosscheck-review.md#6-findings-register)).
2. **Security, before the deviation record is signed.** A robot holding Super Admin breaks the least-privilege control of TISAX (the automotive industry's information-security assessment); the design carries that as a signed deviation record with thirteen compensations, all preconditions of the grant ([../11-tisax.md §6.2](../11-tisax.md#62-the-record--decisions2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-adminmd)). The security reviewer who signs is a person who is not the platform owner, and on 2026-09-18 that role is not filled ([../01-hld.md §0.3](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs)). The security document precedes the appointment, the signature, and the record's re-signature at every Wall-E stage transition.
3. **Compliance, before the classification and before the assessment order.** Under the EU AI Act the Art. 6(4) assessment is written and signed by legal before Stage 1 and registration is due before the first write; no signed assessment, no production, no write ([../10-eu-ai-act.md §3.1.3](../10-eu-ai-act.md#313-art-64-assessment--the-document-its-content-its-owner)). Regulatory dates were re-verified on 2026-09-18 and every date holds ([the cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#51-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-18-with-corrections), section 5.1): the high-risk obligations of Annex III apply from 2027-12-02; the Commission's classification guidelines are still a draft ([../10-eu-ai-act.md §1](../10-eu-ai-act.md#1-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-13)). Any TISAX assessment covering the platform is ordered in 2027 or later, against the ISA2027 catalogue; the last day to order under ISA 6 is 2026-12-31 ([../11-tisax.md §1](../11-tisax.md#1-tisax-on-2026-09-13-verified)), so the ISMS decides before then whether any site order in 2026 lists the platform out of scope.
4. **Architecture, before the factory.** The factory is the pipeline that builds every agent's own cloud project with identity, gateway, logging, budget and deny rules in place; its first run opens Tier R, the read-tools tier, earliest 2026-11-10 to 2026-12-08 ([../setup/README.md §3.4](../setup/README.md#34-parallel-sittings-and-the-critical-path)). One project per agent is forced by Google, not chosen ([../../project-topology.md §1](../../project-topology.md#1-why-four-projects)); the architecture document is read before the first project exists, because a project id is never reusable and a project never moves between tier folders.
5. **The board, before the money.** No price is quoted anywhere in the set; every amount is *tbd*, the design names each cost's driver and payer, and the pricing pages have not been read ([../01-hld.md §0.5](../01-hld.md#05-cost-classes)). No benefit figure exists until four weeks of toil are measured before Wall-E's first phase ([../brief/02-executive-summary.md](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)). The deck and the brief ask for thirteen appointments and thirteen purchase rows, the longest lead time first ([../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md §1](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#1-the-purchase-table-longest-lead-time-first)), and for the two deliberate exits: the stop-or-continue review at the end of Stage 1, and a human approving each action as a legitimate permanent end state.
6. **The pitch, outside the order.** It is used when the approach is presented to someone who has not asked for the design, and it carries the same forbidden sentences as the deck.

## What every document has in common

- It opens with a `## Status` block naming its owner (the platform owner), the date it was last reviewed, who it is for and what it asks of them, and closes with `## Where this is defined`, the links to the pages that hold its facts. The plain-language set uses a three-line Status by design (owner, last reviewed, what the page is), because its front door names the readers once for all ten pages ([../plain/README.md](../plain/README.md#how-every-page-is-laid-out)).
- It names roles, never people: the platform owner, the second human (a human super admin in IT security outside the Wall-E administration line, Eve's owner), the security reviewer, the DPO, the ISMS, the works council.
- It carries no number that does not exist. The counts it may use are the design's own: 6 tiers, 6 containment primitives, 8 kill switches, 13 compensations, 204 decisions, 22 hardware keys (26 with the twin robots — [../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md §4](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#4-hardware-keys-the-count-stated-once-and-the-inventory)), 6 to 7 months to Stage 0 on the full build and not before 2027-03 ([../pov/README.md §4](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)).
- Where a fact is inferred it is marked `Assumption:`; the jurisdiction, the works council's legal basis and the existence of a SOC (security operations centre) or an ISMS in the organisation are all unknown and marked so. On 2026-09-13 the organisation has one administrator, no SOC, no second super admin outside the administrator's line, no security reviewer, no Eve owner, no DPO engaged and no ISMS names supplied ([../01-hld.md §0.3](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs)).
- The two decks follow one slide format so that a renderer turns them into 16:9 pages: a numbered slide heading, at most six short bullets or one small table or one diagram, and presenter notes; the last slide is the ask; an appendix holds the backup slides and the sentences never to be used.

## The honesty rules every document keeps

- **Nothing is built.** Every document describes a design, a procedure or a decision about something that does not exist on 2026-09-18.
- **No price, no benefit figure.** Where a number does not exist the document says so and says what produces it: the four-week toil baseline for the saving, the unread pricing pages for the cost ([../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#what-is-not-yet-priced)).
- **No promised outcome.** The design gives mechanisms and evidence; the regulator, the guidelines and the assessor decide ([../10-eu-ai-act.md §6](../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean)). An assessor may refuse maturity 3 on least privilege for a super-admin robot however good the compensation ([../11-tisax.md §0](../11-tisax.md#0-what-this-page-decides-in-one-paragraph)).
- **Super Admin is designed around, never re-argued.** P33 is the owner's decision of 2026-09-13.
- **Eve's independence is stated as it is.** Inside the organisation it is detective; only the witness organisation — a second Google organisation run by IT security, outside the tenant's super admins' reach — makes it structural ([../01-hld.md §13.2](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human)).
- **The standing constraints, restated exactly wherever restated** ([../01-hld.md#status](../01-hld.md#status)): no domain-wide delegation, ever; no model holds a credential or produces an approval, a signature, a halt, a veto or a refusal; the action service is the only credential holder; a dry run never mutates; humans raise autonomy and machines lower it; Mo reaches production only through a merged pull request.
- **The sentences never to be used**, in any slide, note or script, copied from the build paths that forbid them:
  - "Wall-E is safe as a super admin." — nothing in the proof of value or the three days touches super-admin containment; no agent holds Super Admin in either ([../pov/README.md §1.2](../pov/README.md#12-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message-about-the-pov), [../3-day/README.md §1.1](../3-day/README.md#11-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message)).
  - "Eve is independent." — independence in the design is structural through a witness organisation and a second tenant; both smaller paths have neither, and their Eve lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches (same two sections).
  - "Model Armor blocked the injection." — a probabilistic content screen is never a trust boundary, whatever its grade; the three days send nothing through it and produce no Model Armor evidence at all (same two sections).
  - "We saved `<n>` minutes of admin work." — the three-day doer acted only on synthetic accounts; no human minute was saved ([../3-day/README.md §1.1](../3-day/README.md#11-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message)); the proof of value may not quote minutes saved either ([P196](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204)).
- **House rules.** British English; absolute dates; roles, never people; no personal name and no employer name; `Assumption:` marks an inferred fact and *tbd* a value nobody has decided; a technical term is defined the first time a document uses it.
