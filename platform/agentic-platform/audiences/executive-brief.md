## The ask

The platform owner asks the sponsor for five things, in the order the platform can open:

1. Choose one of three ways to start — three days, the proof of value, or the full build ([the three ways to start](#the-three-ways-to-start)).
2. Appoint, now, a second operator, a security reviewer and a blind grader, and name the grant roles, above all a second human super admin outside the Wall-E line ([who exists and the roles the platform needs](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs)).
3. Start the thirteen purchase rows on day one, the SIEM with its retainer and the witness first, with a named role obtaining each missing price ([the purchase table](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#1-the-purchase-table-longest-lead-time-first); [what is not yet priced](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#what-is-not-yet-priced)).
4. Accept the eight residual risks in writing ([the residual risks a sponsor accepts by signing](../brief/23-threat-model-and-residual-risk.md#the-residual-risks-a-sponsor-accepts-by-signing)).
5. Make the three first signatures possible — the owner's two lists (P29) and purpose (P28), the security reviewer's signature on the deviation record (P33/P136) — by appointing the security reviewer and releasing legal's time ([first actions](../brief/30-decisions-awaiting-owner.md#first-actions)).

No money figure is asked for, because none exists. Three things do not wait: the four-week toil baseline, which cannot be recovered once Wall-E's first phase has run ([why build it, and how it can stop](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)); the ISMS's decision by 2026-12-01 on ordering any assessment under ISA 6 before 2026-12-31 ([TISAX on 2026-09-13](../11-tisax.md#1-tisax-on-2026-09-13-verified)); and the duties of Art. 4, 5 and 50 of the EU AI Act, which bind the organisation as deployer of Gemini Enterprise already ([the regulatory state](../10-eu-ai-act.md#1-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-13)).

### Why

The platform owner asks the organisation to fund and staff a secure environment on Google Cloud and Google Workspace on which agents — an agent being a program that uses a language model to decide what to do, and which may be given tools to act — can run under controls that Google enforces once and one person can own. On it, three agents come first: Wall-E, the doer, an administration assistant that acts on a named operator's request through a dedicated, licensed Workspace user account holding Super Admin, the Workspace role with every administrative power; Eve, the controller, which watches and reports Wall-E's misbehaviour without Super Admin and, as the objective asked, independently of it — a reading the design qualifies under "Eve and Mo" below; and Mo, the improver, which measures both and proposes changes. The objective of 2026-09-13 also asks that the platform sustain hundreds of agents, host agents of general capability in future, deliver enterprise-class security and monitoring, and be "EU AI Act bulletproof" and TISAX compatible ([the objective](../00-objective-review.md#1-the-objective)). Of those readings, two are declined and several qualified: no design can promise a regulator's or an assessor's decision, and agents of general capability get a closed tier until conditions that are out of reach in 2026 are met ([traceability](../01-hld.md#06-traceability-the-objectives-sixteen-requirements-and-the-register-ids-not-cited-elsewhere)). The signature asked for is not a commitment to full autonomy: a human approving each action is a legitimate permanent end state, and a dated stop-or-continue review at the end of Wall-E's first writing stage stops the programme if the balance is negative ([why build it, and how it can stop](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)).

## What is proposed

The platform is five things ([thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis)):

| Thing | What it is, in plain words |
|---|---|
| A folder | A place in the organisation's Google Cloud hierarchy where every control Google can enforce once is set where one person can own it |
| A factory | A pipeline that creates each agent's own cloud project with identity, gateway, logging, budget and deny rules already in place |
| A register | One file in version control that every agent needs a row in before it may exist |
| A contract | The rules every writing agent adopts for autonomy, audit and measurement |
| A gate | A tier of agents opens only when the people and bought services it needs exist |

Agents belong to lettered tiers ([the tier model](../01-hld.md#11-the-tier-model)): **C**, no-code assistants inside the Gemini Enterprise app (Google's enterprise assistant, for which the tenant holds licences), retrieval only, under the user's own permissions; **R**, agents with read-only tools; **W**, agents that write to a system of record through an action service — the deterministic code that holds the credential, decides, audits and executes, so that the model never touches the credential; **P**, agents holding a tenant-wide administrative credential, with **P-SA** the single super-admin agent; and **X**, reserved for agents of general capability and closed. The one human front door is the Gemini Enterprise app. Wall-E, Eve and Mo are the first tenants, proof that the rules can be met, not the platform's definition. The sentence that sizes the whole proposal: "A read-only assistant costs a register row and a factory run. A super-admin robot costs a witness organisation, a second super admin, a bought detection desk and a signed deviation." ([thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis))

The standing constraints, restated exactly as every document in this set restates them, and which no document loosens: no domain-wide delegation, ever; no model holds a credential or produces an approval, a signature, a halt, a veto or a refusal; the action service is the only credential holder; a dry run never mutates; humans raise autonomy and machines lower it; Mo reaches production only through a merged pull request. The design's own list, eight constraints, is in the high-level design's status block ([HLD status](../01-hld.md#status)); the dry-run rule is stated as an absolute of the three-day build ([the nine absolutes](../3-day/README.md#2-the-nine-absolutes)). Giving a robot Super Admin is the owner's decision of 2026-09-13 (P33); the design is built around it and does not re-argue it ([decision record](../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md)).

## The central fact, and the compensations

Two Google facts fix everything and are not negotiable: a service account, the identity a program normally uses, can hold any Workspace admin role except Super Admin, so Wall-E must be a user account; and Super Admin cannot be limited to a part of the organisation or a subset of its powers ([what this reverses and what it costs](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs); recorded on 2026-09-13, to be re-verified). The consequence, stated without softening: the platform does not narrow a super-admin account, because nothing in Workspace or Google Cloud can; Google's only remaining ceiling is the set of permissions the robot's credentials were consented with; a leaked token or an interactive login on the robot is a compromise of the whole Workspace tenant with a path into the Google Cloud organisation; least privilege becomes a signed deviation from TISAX control 4.2.1; and detection, not refusal, is the primary control for the super-admin tier ([what the platform does not do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do)). What makes it acceptable to try is that thirteen compensations are preconditions of the grant, none deferred ([Wall-E on the platform](../01-hld.md#131-wall-e--the-super-admin-singleton-in-tier-p-sa)): three lanes decided by code, of which only a catalogue of typed operations can ever run without a human, while uncatalogued super-admin work needs two different human super admins and console-only work is done by a human; the two signed lists of what is hard-denied and what needs two people (P29); two credentials in two services, with the broad one never used unattended; detection as the primary control, with a bought desk that acknowledges round the clock; account hygiene with Google's multi-party approval switched on, the only Google-enforced two-person rule over the robot; kill switches K0 to K7, of which K6, a human removing the role, is the one that survives a token already issued; a permanent ceiling under which no super-admin-class operation is ever autonomous; a second human outside Wall-E's administration line; the signed record; and the EU AI Act position. The gate checklist adds a penetration test with no open critical or high finding, the data protection impact assessment, works-council information, a crisis tabletop and witnessed custody of the hardware keys ([the checklist the gate reads](../11-tisax.md#63-compensations-as-preconditions--the-checklist-the-gate-reads)). After the grant, any row turning red is a finding with a thirty-day window; two rows red at once, or the detection, hygiene or second-human row red at all, makes the on-duty human super admin pull K6 until they are green: the role exists only while its compensations do.

## Eve and Mo

Eve has two paths ([Eve on the platform](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human)). The control path, which approves, vetoes, halts and demotes, contains no language model by construction: the service that would provide one is denied at project level and the build refuses an image that contains a model client. A separate reporting path may reason about findings, but writes only reports and pages a human at severity 2, and nothing it writes is read by any gate. Eve holds a read-only Workspace role, never Super Admin; any robot event in any log stream without a matching audit row is a halt. From its first run, before Wall-E exists, Eve watches every human super admin and reports to the second human, who is never the subject. Its independence is stated honestly: a super admin can reach anything inside the tenant's Google Cloud organisation, so inside it Eve is detective, not structural; what leaves is structural — the evidence, the incident record and the pager go to a witness organisation, a separate Google organisation run by two people from IT security outside the Wall-E line, which sees the deletion of Eve within fifteen minutes and does not see a forged approval ([Eve's independence](../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness)). Moving Eve's control path into the witness is the proposed end state (P15), not a condition of the first grant. Mo is a body of committed SQL, one deterministic job and, optionally and never load-bearing, one model; every number it publishes is re-derived by a validator Mo cannot reach; it holds no credential, no secret and no signer, and reaches production only through a pull request — a proposed change to configuration — that two humans review, one outside the owner line ([Mo's thesis](../../mo/01-hld.md#thesis)). Eve reads nothing Mo writes; Eve can lower, Mo can only propose.

## The people, the purchases and the order of the gates

**Gates on 2026-09-18.** Tier C and Tier R can open with the people who exist; Tier W, Tier P and the super-admin grant wait on people, bought services and the register's gate group "before the super-admin grant"; Tier X is closed ([the tier gate](../01-hld.md#04-the-tier-gate-what-must-exist-before-a-tier-opens)). The build procedures open Tier R before Tier C, because the tenant-app import needs the factory (P156; [blocks](../setup/README.md#31-blocks)). The super-admin grant is a gate with its own checklist, not a runbook phase: it happens the day the last row is green, and that is the day the platform owner stops owning all four owner groups, which today are one person.

**People, per gate** ([who exists and the roles](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs); [the minimum per stage](../11-tisax.md#72-the-minimum-per-stage)):

|---|---|---|

**Purchases**, thirteen rows, longest lead time first, every row started on day one ([the purchase table](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#1-the-purchase-table-longest-lead-time-first)): (1) a SIEM — the security information and event management service that collects logs, runs detections and has a desk that acknowledges — Google SecOps in the EU or the organisation's own, with a managed detection and response retainer for 24x7 acknowledgement: months (`Assumption:`), blocks Tier P; (2) a penetration test, scoped before the first Tier W write and full before the grant: weeks to months; (3) Security Command Center Premium, Google's organisation-wide security findings service, at organisation level: weeks, blocks Tier C; (4) the witness organisation: a separately registered domain, its own billing account and support subscription: weeks; (5) a sandbox Workspace tenant with two sandbox super admins, because mutating super-admin tests never run on production: weeks; (6) a paging service: weeks if bought, days if IT security runs one; (7) Chrome Enterprise Premium licences, one per operator and approver: weeks; (8) model throughput on the EU location, only if needed; (9) hardware security keys, 22, or 26 with the twin robots: days to weeks; (10) a dedicated billing account in EUR and a quota request for about 20 projects: days; (11) Gemini Enterprise licences and mail-bearing seats for `walle@` and `eve@`: days; (12) a git-host plan with protected branches: weeks; (13) tamper-evident envelopes, a safe with a sign-out log, custody forms: days to weeks. The three-day build buys nothing; the proof of value avoids every long-lead purchase ([what is not on the POV's critical path](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)).

The cost of the gate is speed: "a sponsor who funds the build but not the people gets a read-only platform" ([the tier gate](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#the-tier-gate)).

## The regulatory position and the dates

The platform promises the mechanisms and the evidence, and lists what the regulator, the guidelines or the assessor still decide; it does not promise outcomes ([what "bulletproof" can and cannot mean](../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean); [the TISAX position](../11-tisax.md#0-what-this-page-decides-in-one-paragraph)). Every regulatory fact below was re-verified at its source on 2026-09-18 and holds ([the cross-check review of 2026-09-18, §5.1](../14-crosscheck-review.md#51-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-18-with-corrections)).

**EU AI Act** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744): the obligations that already bind are Art. 4 (AI literacy), Art. 5 (prohibited practices) and Art. 50 (transparency, since 2026-08-02); the high-risk obligations of Annex III apply from 2027-12-02 ([the regulatory state](../10-eu-ai-act.md#1-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-13)). Classification follows the purpose the provider declares, not the privilege an account holds: Wall-E's declared purpose is its catalogue, adjacent to the high-risk category for decisions affecting workers, with the narrow-task derogation claimed, assessed and registered in the EU database before its first write — the register refuses production while the registration is pending ([Wall-E's classification](../10-eu-ai-act.md#31-wall-e-wall-e)). The Super Admin grant is the fact most likely to be held against that reading. The Commission's guidelines on classification are still a draft, expected final by end 2026, and a re-run of every assessment within 90 days of the final text is already armed; no harmonised standard is cited in the Official Journal, so no presumption of conformity exists; on 2026-09-18 the registration duty for systems claiming the derogation was confirmed retained by the co-legislators, which closes the one open legal question in the design's favour ([the cross-check review, §5.1](../14-crosscheck-review.md#51-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-18-with-corrections)). Eve's control path is not an AI system, as an invariant the build enforces; Mo is minimal risk; the organisation is provider and deployer of Wall-E and Mo, and deployer of Google's models ([roles](../10-eu-ai-act.md#2-roles--which-legal-entity-is-provider-and-which-is-deployer)). The legal entity is *tbd* (P23).

**Employees and the works council.** The organisation is the data controller. Eve monitors every human super admin from its first run, and the identity log store copies every account's sign-ins: that is employee monitoring under GDPR Art. 88, whatever the AI Act says about Eve, and it needs the DPO's record before any Eve job runs ([monitoring of administrators, P154](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191)). The register gates works-council information on the first write of any agent acting on employee accounts (P129); on the platform's own order Eve goes live before that, so the information or consultation must come before Eve's first stream, and the DPIA — the data protection impact assessment the DPO owns, the longest lead item in the plan — is read as due before that processing rather than merely started at the grant; the DPO and the owner settle which ([still open](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#229-still-open)). `Assumption:` the jurisdiction is unknown, so whether the works council is informed or consulted, and whether its consent is required, is unknown. The pages' default is information before putting into service, with a consultation record kept where national law requires consultation, marked `Assumption:` ([workers](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129); [brief 26 §22.7](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#227-workers-information-consultation-and-the-explanation-path-p129)). The cross-check review of 2026-09-18 found that default the weaker of the two possibilities and recommends consultation, and consent where co-determination applies, with information as the fallback if HR's answer says so ([the review, §5.4](../14-crosscheck-review.md#54-employee-data-and-the-works-council)); that change to the pages is pending, and HR's answer decides.

**TISAX**, the automotive industry's information-security assessment: a label is earned by a site, never by a platform; the target is the Confidential label (`Assumption:`) at assessment level AL2, maturity 3 on every applicable control, and the scope location is *tbd* ([the target](../11-tisax.md#2-the-target--label-level-scope-catalogue-p133)). Assessments ordered from 2027-01-01 run on VDA ISA2027; the last day to order under ISA 6 is 2026-12-31; the ISMS should say by 2026-12-01 whether any site assessment is ordered before then, since nothing built in 2026 can reach maturity 3. The robot's Super Admin is a signed deviation from control 4.2.1 with three signatories — the owner decides, a security reviewer who is not the owner signs, the ISMS enters it — reviewed quarterly, re-signed at every Wall-E stage transition, expiring with the three-year result; an assessor may still refuse maturity 3 on it however good the compensation ([the record](../11-tisax.md#62-the-record--decisions2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-adminmd)). Google's own TISAX result covers its regions, `europe-west1` among them, and names no individual service; per-service coverage is closed from the ENX result share, never from a public page (P32, P135).

## The three ways to start

The three paths form one chain — two people for three days; three hands-on people and one engineer, with POV-1 ending 6 to 9 weeks after day one and POV-2 ending in week 16 to 20 with a dedicated engineer, or 26 to 34 weeks if one person writes the code and runs the procedures; the full build, 6 to 7 months to Stage 0 — and every artefact uses the full build's names and schemas, so nothing is torn down to move on ([how it grows](../3-day/README.md#12-how-it-grows); [the POV's two stages](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)).

| Path | People and effort | What it proves | What it can never claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| The three-day build ([3-day](../3-day/README.md)) | Two people, both Workspace super admins, three business days, six person-days; a sponsor named in writing who does no work; buys nothing, signs no contract | Eve live over the admin audit log before any doer exists; a doer holding one organisational-unit-scoped privilege over four synthetic accounts, a forced dry run, a two-person approved execution; two kill levers pulled and timed; Mo's scorecard against a retrospective baseline; a same-day unwind | Super-admin containment, a witness, a SIEM, a penetration test, a DPIA, a minute saved: it "is a demonstration of machinery under control, not compliance evidence and not a production grant" ([what three days cannot buy](../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy)) |

Three facts a sponsor choosing the smaller paths must hear first. The three-day build's twenty-eight corrections were all made on 2026-09-18 in its day files and code, with a dated record ([corrections made on 2026-09-18](../3-day/README.md#61-corrections-made-on-2026-09-18)), so nothing is owed before the run; its clock is 375 minutes allocated and 75 in reserve on days 1 and 2, 390 and 60 on day 3, which is 38 person-hours allocated and 7 reserved for two people ([status](../3-day/README.md#status)); and its day-3 unwind now removes both OAuth clients (the application records under which the robot consented), their secrets and the standing grants the same day ([the unwind](../3-day/README.md#10-the-unwind-before-anything-real-is-touched)). Second, the cross-check review of 2026-09-18 still refuses its live block as written on three points — the halt lever is writable by the process it halts, a caller-account fallback lets one person request and approve, and its calling path depends on a gcloud behaviour the full build's own page says does not work for user credentials — each a page to change before the run, not a flaw in the stance ([the review, §4](../14-crosscheck-review.md#4-security)). Third, because its Eve reads the production tenant's audit log for every administrator from day one, the DPO's record on monitoring named administrators and HR's answer must exist before day one, or the poll is limited to the two participants with their written consent ([the review, §5.4](../14-crosscheck-review.md#54-employee-data-and-the-works-council)).

## The risks a signature accepts

In order of consequence ([the residual risks a sponsor accepts by signing](../brief/23-threat-model-and-residual-risk.md#the-residual-risks-a-sponsor-accepts-by-signing)):

1. **A stolen robot credential is a takeover of the Workspace tenant and a route into the cloud organisation.** Custody, detection within minutes and people pulling switches meet it; nothing prevents it; a token can work for up to an hour after suspension (`Assumption:`). It is risk row R-01, accepted with compensation, and its signature is pending because the security reviewer role is unfilled ([the risk register](../11-tisax.md#10-the-risk-register-p140)).
2. **Inside the organisation, the controller watches a super admin but cannot structurally stop one.** Only what the witness holds is beyond a super admin's reach; the end state that closes the gap is proposed, not built.
3. **The safety of the design depends on people who do not exist yet.** Tiers that need a second person stay closed until that person exists: a delay, not a control failure.
4. **The robot's gate is one process its build pipeline can rewrite.** Beneath a compromised pipeline only Google's own audit log and detection remain.
5. **Manipulated content will reach the model.** Screening is detection-grade; the ceilings in code are the enforcement.
6. **The regulator and the assessor decide outcomes.** The derogation may be read narrowly; maturity 3 on least privilege may be refused despite the signed deviation.
7. **Some Google facts are unverified** and are kept out of every safety case until a test answers them.
8. **Accepted operational limits**: one region, Google-side tenancy segregation the platform cannot see, some stores on Google-managed keys, one model family across the fleet.

Two further facts belong beside the signature. On 2026-09-18 the deviation record could not be signed by a security reviewer: rows 5 (the SIEM with 24x7 acknowledgement, and the witness), 8 (the perimeter, which waits on the P3 spike, a test on a throwaway resource) and 11 (the second human) of the gate checklist are not green, and rows 1, 2, 4, 9 and 10 cannot be proven while the service code does not exist ([the cross-check review of 2026-09-18, §4](../14-crosscheck-review.md#4-security); [the checklist](../11-tisax.md#63-compensations-as-preconditions--the-checklist-the-gate-reads)). And the count of distinct humans is the gate, not a promise: on 2026-09-13 every role is one person ([the minimum per stage](../11-tisax.md#72-the-minimum-per-stage)).
