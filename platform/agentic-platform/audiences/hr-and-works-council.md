- Last reviewed: 2026-09-18

## 1. What is proposed, in the words HR uses

The organisation runs its mail, calendar, documents and employee directory on Google Workspace. The organisation's own instance of it is called the tenant. The person who administers the tenant holds a role Google calls **Super Admin**: every administrative power, which Google cannot limit to part of the organisation or to a subset of its powers ([../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs)).

An **agent** is a program that uses a language model to decide what to do, and may be given tools to act ([../brief/02-executive-summary.md#what-was-asked-and-the-readings-the-design-declines-or-qualifies](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#what-was-asked-and-the-readings-the-design-declines-or-qualifies)). The proposal is a platform on Google Cloud that hosts agents under one set of controls, and three agents as its first tenants ([../01-hld.md#01-thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis)):

- **Wall-E, the doer.** An administration assistant for the tenant. On a named operator's request, or on a catalogued trigger, it reads directory, licence, group and audit data and carries out a fixed list of administrative operations: group membership, profile and organisational-unit fields, licence assignment, labels on its own mailbox, templated notifications, and the suspension or restoration of an account **only when HR or a named operator has already decided**. It never decides who leaves, who is promoted, who is monitored or how anyone performs, and never selects a person by behaviour ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on](../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on)). By the platform owner's decision of 2026-09-13 (P33), it works through a dedicated, licensed user account `walle@` that holds Super Admin ([../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md](../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md)). This document does not re-argue that decision; it says what is designed around it. A P-number is a row of the register of decisions ([../12-open-decisions.md](../12-open-decisions.md)); SD- and PV- rows are its setup and proof-of-value entries; M- rows are Mo's own register.
- **Eve, the controller.** Deterministic rules with no language model on any path that decides, halts or pages a human; a separate report-only component may reason (section 3). Eve watches the tenant's administrative activity, reports what it finds to a human who is not the person watched, and, once Wall-E exists, can stop Wall-E. Eve never holds Super Admin and never writes to an employee's account ([../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human)).
- **Mo, the improver.** Committed SQL (database queries) and one scheduled job that measure how Wall-E and Eve perform and propose changes; a proposal reaches production only through a pull request, a proposed change that two named humans review before it is merged ([../01-hld.md#133-mo--improving-both-one-mo-per-platform-without-touching-eves-independence](../01-hld.md#133-mo--improving-both-one-mo-per-platform-without-touching-eves-independence)).

Two facts fix the shape for HR. First, no model ever holds a credential or produces an approval, a signature, a halt, a veto or a refusal; the **action service**, deterministic code that holds the credential, is the only thing that touches an account ([../01-hld.md#status](../01-hld.md#status)). Second, humans raise an agent's autonomy and machines lower it: no program ever widens what Wall-E may do on its own (same section).

Employees meet the agents through one door, the Gemini Enterprise app, Google's chat front end where an operator types a request ([../README.md#what-the-platform-is](../README.md#what-the-platform-is)). Employees who are not operators meet Wall-E only as the subject of an administrative act, or as the recipient of a notification that says it comes from an AI system.

## 2. Which agent touches employee accounts, how, and with whose approval

Only Wall-E writes to employee accounts. Eve reads administrative activity and writes nothing. Mo reads Wall-E's audit records through pseudonyms and writes nothing ([../../mo/01-hld.md#thesis](../../mo/01-hld.md#thesis)).

**Autonomy levels.** Every operation Wall-E can perform sits at a level, and the level is data a human sets ([../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#2-the-six-levels](../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#2-the-six-levels)):

| Level | Meaning for an employee's account |
|---|---|
| L0 | Off: the operation is refused. |
| L1 | Shadow: a **dry run** that records what would have happened and never changes anything, even when handed a valid approval. |
| L2 | Propose: Wall-E writes a proposal for a human; there is no execution path at all. |
| L3 | Human: a named operator approves each action before it runs; the approval expires after 4 business hours. |
| L4 | Eve: Wall-E may act after a hold window in which a human can veto; Eve, not a model, gives the technical approval. |
| L5 | Auto: Wall-E acts within scope; Eve verifies independently within 60 minutes; humans read a digest. |

Triggers are four: T0 an operator's chat request, T1 a schedule, T2 an event from another system, T3 the robot's own inbox ([../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#stage-overview](../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#stage-overview)). Levels climb on evidence through six stages, S0 Eyes (read-only, at least 3 to 4 weeks) to S5 Steady state; the durations are floors, not a plan (same section). Before the Super Admin grant the robot exists, is licensed and hardened, holds no admin role and reads nothing ([../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#the-super-admin-grant--a-gate-not-a-stage](../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#the-super-admin-grant--a-gate-not-a-stage)).

**The catalogue, family by family**, with the highest level the design allows and the human decision that precedes each ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#312-annex-iii-4b-family-by-family](../10-eu-ai-act.md#312-annex-iii-4b-family-by-family); [../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#5-operation-families](../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#5-operation-families)):

| Family | What it does to an employee | Highest level | Which human decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Observe | Reads directory, licence, group and audit data; weekly digests (last week's changes, accounts unused for 90 days, suspended accounts still licensed, groups with external members or no owner) | L5 | Reports reach a human and never a write plan |
| F2 / F2b Notify | Templated notices to fixed recipients; free-text mail or chat that the action service marks as coming from an AI system | L4 / L2 | Free text is approved by an operator, every time |
| F3 / F3b Groups | Adds or removes membership of ordinary groups; access groups separately | L4 / L3 | Explicit targets named by an operator; security groups blocked |
| F4 / F4b Profile, OU | Changes profile fields and moves an account between organisational units (OUs, the folders the directory is organised in) | L4 / L3 | Explicit targets; an allow-list of destination OUs |
| **F5 Suspend** | Suspends an account, the technical consequence of a leaver or leave decision | **L4 only on the HR system of record's event; L3 on chat or schedule** | HR or a named operator has already decided; the plan cites the decision record |
| F6 Restore | Restores a suspended account | L3, permanently | A human approves each restoration |
| F7-suspended Licences | Removes licences from accounts already suspended | as F5 | Nobody is chosen by Wall-E |
| F7-inactive Licences | Removes licences from active accounts on a list a human supplied | L3 until P18 is answered; selection never automated | The human who wrote the list |
| Band B Uncatalogued | Any other administrative request, fully specified by one human super administrator | L3, permanently | A second human super administrator approves; a natural-person target needs the decision record or the lane refuses |
| Band C Console-only | Steps written for a human to carry out in the Admin console | none | A human does the work |

In HR's vocabulary: joiners are F3, F4 and F7 (groups, profile, OU, licence); leavers are F5 then F7-suspended; suspensions are F5; licence reclaim is F7. The first writing stage, S1, is where leaver and joiner actions from chat and licence reclaim from suspended accounts begin ([../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)). An autonomous suspension (L4) is possible only on the event from the HR system of record, and waits in a hold that a human can veto and that never expires outside business hours ([../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#224-the-actions-that-affect-employees-and-how-they-are-bounded](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#224-the-actions-that-affect-employees-and-how-they-are-bounded)).

**What an employee who is not an operator will see.** Nothing, in the ordinary case: an account attribute changes and the person notices only if they look. Where Wall-E sends a message (family F2b), the action service, never the model, prepends a fixed line stating that the message comes from an AI system, appends a signature block and sets a fixed mail header; a message without the disclosure is refused (`disclosure_missing`). This is the EU AI Act's Art. 50 duty, in force since 2026-08-02, and it is built so that no prompt can remove it ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#47-art-50--disclosure-on-outbound-messages-on-the-surface-and-the-marking-position-p128](../10-eu-ai-act.md#47-art-50--disclosure-on-outbound-messages-on-the-surface-and-the-marking-position-p128)). Free-text messages stay at L2: an operator approves every one before it leaves.

**Two smaller builds write to synthetic accounts only.** The proof of value (three hands-on people and one engineer; 6 to 9 weeks to its first stage, week 16 to 20 to its second, `Assumption:`) acts only on a pilot OU of synthetic accounts (`Assumption:` 5 to 10) that receive no real mail and hold no real data; its doer holds no Workspace admin role at all; a real account enters only with a completed and signed DPIA, HR's written answer on works-council information or consultation, and the holder's written consent ([../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204), P197). The three-day build (two people, three business days, `Assumption:` 2026-09-22 to 2026-09-24) acts on four synthetic accounts, its doer holds one OU-scoped privilege, and the procedure refuses to run if a real account is in the pilot OU ([../3-day/README.md#2-the-nine-absolutes](../3-day/README.md#2-the-nine-absolutes)). Both build paths list sentences that may never appear in any report about them; the two that concern this audience are quoted in section 3.

## 3. Eve watches the human super administrators, and that is monitoring of employees

On the platform owner's request of 2026-09-15, Eve watches **every human super administrator and every live holder of an admin role** from its first run, before Wall-E exists: the platform owner included ([../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human)). Eve reads the **admin audit log**, Google's record of every administrative act in the tenant, together with the login, token, SAML (the single-sign-on protocol), groups and Reports activity streams, Google's records of activity in the tenant; the purpose is detection of misuse of tenant-wide privilege ([../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191), P154).

The pages say what this is: **employee monitoring**. The administrators are employees; the data is metadata about their actions at work; GDPR Art. 88(2) names "monitoring systems at the work place" (confirmed against the text of the Regulation on 2026-09-18; [the cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#5-the-path-to-the-regulation), section 5). The design therefore blocks Eve's polling and reconciliation jobs until a DPO record exists that states purpose, data categories, retention, recipients, and the works-council information given where the jurisdiction requires it ([../setup/24-eve-workspace-identity-and-audit-feeds.md#ew-02-check-sd-11s-data-protection-record-before-any-feed-exists](../setup/24-eve-workspace-identity-and-audit-feeds.md#ew-02-check-sd-11s-data-protection-record-before-any-feed-exists)). The record's content, as the pages fix it:

- **Data:** admin, login, token, SAML, groups and Reports activity metadata. **No content.** Eve's Workspace role is read-only and carries no content scope ([../../eve/01-hld.md#what-eve-is-not](../../eve/01-hld.md#what-eve-is-not)).
- **Subjects:** every account on the super-admin roster and every live admin-role holder; `walle@` joins that set only when it exists.
- **Retention:** the `eve_workspace_*` datasets at the DPO's number (400 days is the floor, P13 is open) and a copy in the witness organisation, a separate Google organisation run by IT security outside the tenant's reach ([../brief/31-glossary.md#platform-terms](../brief/31-glossary.md#platform-terms)).
- **Recipients:** the second human (a super administrator in IT security outside Wall-E's administration line), the security reviewer, the incident commander. **Never the subject.** A finding about the platform owner goes only to the second human ([../../eve/01-hld.md#scope-from-the-first-run-the-human-super-admins-2026-09-15](../../eve/01-hld.md#scope-from-the-first-run-the-human-super-admins-2026-09-15)); reports about the second human go to the security reviewer (setup file 26), a role nobody yet holds (BLOCKED row B-12; the incident commander until one is appointed) ([../setup/README.md#8-blocked-index](../setup/README.md#8-blocked-index)).

What it means for the people watched. Eve's rules are written by humans, fixed in code, and each finding names the rule that fired; no model reasons about a person on the path that pages a human ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#32-eve-control-path--not-an-ai-system-as-an-invariant-eve](../10-eu-ai-act.md#32-eve-control-path--not-an-ai-system-as-an-invariant-eve)). The one reporting component that may reason, `eve-advisor`, is report-only, pages at severity 2 only, and names no human without the deterministic rule behind the finding until legal classifies it (P19, open) ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#33-eve-advisor--class-tbd-p19-interim-rules-eve-advisor](../10-eu-ai-act.md#33-eve-advisor--class-tbd-p19-interim-rules-eve-advisor)). The design records the chilling effect of monitoring administrators as a risk to fundamental rights, with no residual stated until P19 is answered ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement](../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement)). Who watches the watcher: findings about the platform owner go only to the second human, and the evidence copy is held outside the tenant by IT security ([../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness](../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness)).

**The three-day build and the proof of value.** In both, Eve has no witness organisation and lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches, so both forbid the sentence quoted below. The proof of value gates Eve's first run on the DPO record with HR's works-council half: requested on day one and signed before Eve's first run (POV weeks 2 to 5, `Assumption:`); no Eve step starts without it ([../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204), P199; [../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)). The three-day build, as written on 2026-09-18, polls the production tenant's admin audit log for every actor from its first hour with no DPO record and no works-council answer; its own pages list "no DPIA and no works-council information" among the things three days cannot buy ([../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy](../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy); [the cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#5-the-path-to-the-regulation), section 5). HR should therefore treat the three-day build as it treats the full build: no poll of real administrators' activity before the DPO record and HR's answer exist, or a poll limited to the two participants with their written consent.

The sentences never to be used, with the reason each page gives. From the three-day build's page ([../3-day/README.md#11-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message](../3-day/README.md#11-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message)):

> 2. 'Eve is independent.' Independence in the design is structural: a witness organisation, a second tenant. This set has neither (C-01), and its Eve lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches.

> 4. 'We saved `<n>` minutes of admin work.' The doer acted only on synthetic accounts. No human minute was saved, and Mo's scorecard says so in bold (C-07).

From the proof of value's page, in its own words ([../pov/README.md#12-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message-about-the-pov](../pov/README.md#12-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message-about-the-pov)); the proof of value has no item 4, and it may not quote minutes saved by a separate rule (P196):

> 2. 'Eve is independent.' Independence in the design is structural (a witness organisation, a second tenant). The POV has neither (PV-D-03), and its Eve lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches.

## 4. What data about employees each agent processes, where it is kept, for how long, who reads it

The organisation is the **controller**: the platform administers its own tenant, so the frame is the existing GDPR programme, not a separate regime for AI ([../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#221-the-position-controller-and-internal-processing](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#221-the-position-controller-and-internal-processing)). All stores are in the EU (`europe-west1` or the BigQuery `EU` multi-region; the Gemini Enterprise app in the `eu` location) ([../../project-topology.md#2-the-four-projects](../../project-topology.md#2-the-four-projects)). Retention values are the proposal of the retention schedule; the DPO sets the ceiling per class before the first write (P13, open) ([../12-open-decisions.md#3-before-any-tier-w-agent-writes](../12-open-decisions.md#3-before-any-tier-w-agent-writes)). Two readers recur in the table: the **SIEM**, the security information and event management service that collects logs, runs detections and has a desk that acknowledges alerts, bought for the privileged tier; and the **security reviewer**, an IT security person who signs off controls and is never the platform owner.

| Store | Agent | Whose data, what | Kept | Who can read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Directory, licence, group and audit reads | Wall-E | Every employee's directory record, licence, group memberships and admin events, from the read-only stage S0; the last-sign-in report for the inactivity digest | Not stored by Wall-E beyond the audit row and the digest | Operators, through the Gemini Enterprise app |
| Audit row and frozen plan (`walle_audit`) | Wall-E | Who requested (as a pseudonym), who approved, what changed, on whom; designated the EU AI Act Art. 12 log | 400 days floor; a daily copy in the evidence bucket and in the witness | Wall-E's owners and operators through their log view; Eve's read-only accounts; the security reviewer; the SIEM ([../08-data-logging-retention-sovereignty.md#33-log-views-and-who-reads-what](../08-data-logging-retention-sovereignty.md#33-log-views-and-who-reads-what)) |
| Content logs and raw copy | Wall-E | Full prompt text, names, addresses, the operator's address | 30 days, a ceiling | One view, readable by Wall-E's operators group and IT security only; the prompt fields are hidden from anyone else who reaches it ([../08-data-logging-retention-sovereignty.md#5-the-retention-schedule-g52](../08-data-logging-retention-sovereignty.md#5-the-retention-schedule-g52)); graders read only the de-identified copy |
| De-identified copy | Wall-E, Mo | The same with identifiers replaced by tokens; free text kept | 30 days (90 only if the DPO approves) | Blind graders, Mo's metrics identity |
| Conversation history | Gemini Enterprise app | Operators' prompts and answers, which name employees; keyed on the user | 30 days interim (`Assumption:` until the DPO decides under P13); never exported to the evidence stores | The user; the pages state the retention and the DPIA entry, not a further reader list ([../03-gemini-enterprise-environment.md#14-retention-and-personal-data-of-the-front-door](../03-gemini-enterprise-environment.md#14-retention-and-personal-data-of-the-front-door)) |
| `StreamAssist` Data Access entry | Gemini Enterprise app | The only log naming the human who asked | 400 days | The Gemini Enterprise administrator, the security reviewer, the SIEM |
| Identity log store | Platform | Sign-in, application-token and single-sign-on entries of **every** tenant account: the densest personal-data store; legal basis legitimate interest, `Assumption:` the DPO confirms | 400 days, `Assumption:`; the row where the DPO's ceiling most plausibly differs | The security reviewer, Eve's read-only account, the SIEM; **no agent owner, no operator** |
| `eve_workspace_*` and `eve.incidents` | Eve | Activity metadata of every super administrator and admin-role holder; findings | 400 days floor; a copy in the witness | The second human, the security reviewer, the incident commander; never the subject |
| Mo's metrics | Mo | Aggregates over pseudonyms only, minimum cell size 5 (`Assumption:`) | 400 days | Reader set open (M-3), including whether the works council sees it ([../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#226-operators-as-data-subjects-the-limits-on-monitoring](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#226-operators-as-data-subjects-the-limits-on-monitoring)) |
| Pseudonym mapping | Mo | The table that links a pseudonym to a person | Life of the agent; deleted by `revoke` | Nobody; secret class |
| Explanation-request log | HR, Wall-E | Each employee's request for an explanation and its answer | 400 days per the evidence register; not yet in the retention schedule | HR; the DPO |
| SIEM export of Workspace events | Platform (from Tier P) | Google's native export of every supported event type, Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Chat and Meet event streams included: events, not content; configured by a human administrator, never the robot | At least 400 days in the SIEM | The detection desk, IT security ([../07-monitoring-detection-incident-response.md#11-the-four-organisation-owned-feeds-and-where-each-lands](../07-monitoring-detection-incident-response.md#11-the-four-organisation-owned-feeds-and-where-each-lands)) |

The full walk of one request, store by store, is [../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#223-where-a-person-is-identifiable-along-one-request](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#223-where-a-person-is-identifiable-along-one-request). A data-subject request against a locked evidence store is answered by disclosure, not deletion; minimisation keeps that lawful, because locked stores hold only who requested, who approved, what changed and on whom ([../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#222-what-the-platform-owes-the-dpo-and-where-each-input-stands](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#222-what-the-platform-owes-the-dpo-and-where-each-input-stands)).

**What is not read.** Precisely what the sources say:

- **Mail and document content of employees.** Wall-E's permission list carries Gmail scopes for **its own mailbox only** (reading its own inbox as a trigger, labelling its own mail, sending); the `drive` scope is not requested because without domain-wide delegation the robot can only see its own Drive; the Vault API, which would read all retained content in the tenant, is refused as "not worth it" ([../../wall-e/02-identity-and-auth.md](../../wall-e/02-identity-and-auth.md)). **Domain-wide delegation**, the Workspace mechanism that lets a program act as any user, is granted to nothing, ever ([../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do)). Eve's monitoring is metadata with no content scope. The honest caveat the pages carry: Super Admin behind the account means the consented permission list is the only Google-enforced ceiling, and a leaked token would disclose tenant-wide what those read permissions reach ([../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs)).
- **Gemini Enterprise conversation history.** Kept 30 days interim, keyed on the user, not exported to the evidence stores; the audit of what an operator asked is the audit row and the Data Access entry, not the chat; a change of identity provider deletes it; the Gemini Enterprise baseline never lowers retention below today's value without notice to users at least one retention period ahead ([../03-gemini-enterprise-environment.md#54-conversation-retention-p52-under-p13](../03-gemini-enterprise-environment.md#54-conversation-retention-p52-under-p13); [../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191), P163).
- **Behaviour and performance.** No agent reads how anyone works; see section 5.

## 5. No performance monitoring, no profiling, no automated decision with legal effect on a person

These are the design's positions, stated as such; a supervisory authority's reading is not promised.

- **No performance monitoring.** Wall-E's declared purpose says it never decides who is monitored or how anyone performs ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on](../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on)). Operators are measured once: each grades, blind, a monthly sample of their own approvals, and a fall below the threshold opens a review item, **never an automatic consequence for a person**; an overseer without a training record leaves the operator group as a missing qualification, never as performance ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#46-art-14-and-art-262--oversight-designed-in-overseers-named-caps-per-family-p127](../10-eu-ai-act.md#46-art-14-and-art-262--oversight-designed-in-overseers-named-caps-per-family-p127)).
- **No profiling.** Selecting accounts for licence reclaim by sign-in and usage would evaluate behaviour at work, and inactivity heuristics foreseeably disadvantage part-time staff and people on leave, off sick or disabled. So F7 is split: **no AI output selects a person by behaviour**; the inactivity report is a read artefact for a human briefed on that bias; targets come only from ids a human supplied; both the build pipeline and the action service refuse report-derived targets. Whether inactivity reclaim is profiling in law is P18, open with legal and the DPO; if the answer is "profiling", the design recommends removing `F7-inactive` rather than reclassifying Wall-E ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#314-the-profiling-question-answered-by-design-p126](../10-eu-ai-act.md#314-the-profiling-question-answered-by-design-p126)).
- **No solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effect (GDPR Art. 22).** The design's position: the suspension decision is HR's or a named operator's, and Wall-E executes it citing the decision record; licence reclaim from active accounts takes a human-supplied list; the families that may run at L5 (groups, fields, OU moves) produce no legal or similarly significant effect ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129)). The point a works council will press, and the pages' honest answer: at L4 on the HR event the *action* executes without a human looking at *that* action; the residual is that a wrongful suspension (wrong homonym, stale HR feed, injected instruction) can last the hold window plus the restore's human approval, up to four business hours ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement](../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement)).
- **Under the EU AI Act**, Wall-E is classed next to, not inside, the high-risk list for employment; the assessment is signed by legal before the first write ([compliance-and-regulation.md#4-classification-of-each-system](compliance-and-regulation.md#4-classification-of-each-system)). The high-risk obligations for that list bind from 2027-12-02; the design adopts the worker-information duty now ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean](../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean)).

**Operators and approvers are employees too.** The people who type requests and approve actions are data subjects of the platform ([../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#226-operators-as-data-subjects-the-limits-on-monitoring](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#226-operators-as-data-subjects-the-limits-on-monitoring)):

- Their prompts sit in the conversation history and the content logs (30 days), their identity in every audit row as a pseudonym and in the one Data Access entry that names them (400 days).
- Their competence and training records qualify them as named overseers under the EU AI Act; the record is refreshed at each stage transition.
- The blind self-grading sample is the platform's one measurement of individual judgement; its threshold is *tbd* (Mo owner) and a fall below it opens a review item, never a consequence for the person.
- A per-operator agreement metric is, by Mo's own rule, personal data even when published as an aggregate; who may read it, and whether the works council sees it, is an open decision (M-3).
- Reports about an administrator's own actions go only to the second human, never to the person concerned.

**How it is stopped.** Kill switches are pre-built ways to stop an agent ([../../wall-e/ARCHITECTURE.md#46-kill-switches](../../wall-e/ARCHITECTURE.md#46-kill-switches)). Any operator or Eve can halt every write at once; only a human can remove the robot's Super Admin; no model can pull any switch; the security document holds the switches and their targets ([security-team.md#3-the-kill-switches-k0k7](security-team.md#3-the-kill-switches-k0k7)). Beyond switches, the programme has two deliberate exits: a dated stop-or-continue review at the end of S1, and L3, a human approving each action, as a legitimate permanent end state ([../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)).

## 10. Jurisdiction and the legal basis of co-determination

`Assumption:` **the jurisdiction is unknown.** Which national labour and works-council law applies is *tbd*, because the operating site's country is not fixed, and no page names one ([../11-tisax.md#11-the-legal-and-contractual-register-p141](../11-tisax.md#11-the-legal-and-contractual-register-p141)). Three consequences for this meeting, each marked as an assumption:

- `Assumption:` where national law makes the introduction of a technical device capable of monitoring employees' behaviour or performance subject to co-determination, the works council's **consent** is required, the usual instrument is a works agreement, and its lead time is months rather than weeks. Eve over the administrators and the identity log store are such devices from their first stream, whatever the AI Act says.
- `Assumption:` where the law provides information and consultation, the council is consulted before a technology affecting working conditions or monitoring is introduced. The pages' default of information, with a consultation record where required, is the weaker of the two possibilities; HR's written answer to the D7 letter is what settles it.
- The proof of value's interim, Eve's first run limited to its two participants with their written consent, is a valid fallback under either route ([../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204)).

**If the answer is no.** If HR's answer is that consultation or consent is required and it is not obtained, no Eve feed over administrators is deployed (P154, [../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191)) and Stage 1 of any agent acting on employee accounts does not open (P129, [../12-open-decisions.md#3-before-any-tier-w-agent-writes](../12-open-decisions.md#3-before-any-tier-w-agent-writes)); the platform stays at its read-only tiers, a state the design treats as legitimate: "a sponsor who funds the build but not the people gets a read-only platform" ([../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#the-tier-gate](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#the-tier-gate)).

The legal entity that acts as provider under the EU AI Act and that the employee notice must name is also open (P23) ([../10-eu-ai-act.md#2-roles--which-legal-entity-is-provider-and-which-is-deployer](../10-eu-ai-act.md#2-roles--which-legal-entity-is-provider-and-which-is-deployer)).
