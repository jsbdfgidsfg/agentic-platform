## Vocabulary the scripts rely on

The glossary of record is [../brief/31-glossary.md](../brief/31-glossary.md). The terms below are defined here because the scripts use them before a listener could look them up.

- **Agent**: a program that uses a language model to decide what to do, and may be given tools to act.
- **Super Admin**: the Google Workspace role with every administrative power, which Google cannot limit to part of the organisation or to a subset of its powers ([what this reverses](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs); recorded 2026-09-13, to be re-verified).
- **Action service**: deterministic code that holds the credential, decides, audits and executes, so that the model never touches the credential.
- **Dry run**: an execution mode that records what would have happened and never mutates, even when handed a valid approval.
- **Pull request**: a proposed change to configuration that named humans review before it is merged.
- **Kill switch**: a pre-built way to stop an agent, from halting its writes (K0) to a human removing its Super Admin (K6) to stopping the whole fleet (K7).
- **Witness organisation**: a second Google organisation run by IT security, outside the reach of the tenant's super admins, holding the controller's evidence copy, incident record and pager.
- **Works council**: the employees' elected representatives.
- **Tier**: the letter (C, R, W, P, P-SA, X) that says what an agent may do and therefore which controls, people and purchases must exist before it runs.

## The thirty-second pitch

> We are designing a secure environment on Google Cloud and Google Workspace where agents run under controls Google enforces once. Three agents come first: a doer that carries out administration chores a named person asked for; a controller with no language model in its decision path; and an improver that can only propose. The model never holds a credential. Nothing is built; every claim carries what it cannot claim.

## The two-minute pitch

> Every Workspace tenant has an administrator doing the same chores every week: joiners, leavers, groups, licences. We are designing a platform on which a program can do those chores on the record, and which lets hundreds of such programs exist without each becoming a new risk.
>
> The platform is five things: a folder where every control Google enforces once is set where one person owns it; a factory that builds each agent's project with its controls in place; a register every agent needs a row in before it may exist; a contract for autonomy, audit and measurement; and a gate: a tier opens only when its people and purchases exist.
>
> Three agents come first. Wall-E, the doer, acts on a named operator's request through a dedicated, licensed Workspace account that holds Super Admin — the owner's decision, designed around, never re-argued. Eve, the controller, watches every super admin from its first run, before Wall-E exists, and approves, halts or demotes with no language model in that path. Mo, the improver, measures both and reaches production only through a pull request two humans merge.
>
> Nothing is built: the design and a register of 204 decisions exist; no folder, project, robot or log copy does. No price is quoted and no saving is claimed, because the pricing pages have not been read and the four-week toil baseline has not been measured. Three ways to start: two people for three days; three people for six to nine weeks to a first stage and week 16 to 20 to a second, 26 to 34 with one person; or the full build, six to seven months to the first read-only stage, not before March 2027.

## The ten-minute pitch

> **The problem, in one minute.** A Workspace tenant is administered by a few people holding a role that can do anything. Agents — programs that use a language model to decide and may be given tools to act — are arriving in every enterprise, and the temptation is to give one that role and a chat window. Nothing in Workspace or Google Cloud can narrow a super-admin account: a service account can hold any admin role except Super Admin, and Super Admin cannot be limited to one organisational unit or a subset of its powers. So the question is not "how do we make the robot's role small". The question is "what has to be true around a robot that holds a role nobody can shrink, and around the hundred agents that will follow it with smaller roles".
>
> **What the platform is, in two minutes.** Five things. A **folder** in the Google Cloud hierarchy where every control Google can enforce once — rules that refuse an action to every agent regardless of what a project grants — is set where one person can own it. A **factory**, a pipeline that creates each agent's own project with identity, gateway, logging, budget and deny rules in place; one project per agent is forced by Google, not chosen. A **register**: one file in version control that every agent needs a row in before it may exist, and the row carries the agent's tier, its purpose, its EU AI Act class and its ceilings. A **contract**: autonomy is data, not a mood — six levels an operation climbs on evidence, never on the calendar, with humans raising and machines lowering. And a **gate**: agents belong to lettered tiers, C for no-code assistants that only retrieve, R for read tools, W for writing to a system of record through an action service, P for a tenant-wide admin credential, P-SA for the one super-admin robot, and X, closed, for agents of general capability; a tier opens only when the people and bought services it needs exist. The three agents are the platform's first tenants, not its definition.
>
> **The three agents, in three minutes.** *Wall-E* is an administration assistant. On a named operator's request or a catalogued trigger it observes directory, licence, group and audit data and executes catalogued, explicitly targeted operations: group membership, profile fields, organisational-unit moves, licence assignment, templated notices, and suspension or restoration of an account only when HR or a named operator has already decided. It never decides who leaves, who is promoted or how anyone performs; it never selects people by their behaviour; it never changes the tenant's security posture, deletes data, assigns admin roles or spends money. Its credential lives in an action service, never in the model. It has three lanes: catalogued work that can earn autonomy; uncatalogued super-admin work that one human super admin fully specifies and a second approves, permanently at a level where a human approves each action; and console-only work, which becomes instructions to a human — the Admin console is never automated under the robot's session. Giving it Super Admin was the owner's decision on 2026-09-13. The design names, before any compensation, what that costs: the Workspace-side gate is lost, a leaked token is a compromise of the tenant, least privilege becomes a signed deviation, and detection becomes the primary control. Thirteen compensations are preconditions of the grant, none deferred, and after the grant the role exists only while its compensations do: certain rows turning red make the on-duty human super admin remove the role until they are green.
>
> *Eve* is the controller. Its control path — the gate that approves, vetoes, halts and demotes — contains no language model by construction: the service that would provide one is denied at project level and the build refuses an image that contains a model client. Eve holds a read-only Workspace role, never Super Admin. Any robot event in any log stream with no matching audit row is a halt. From its first run, before Wall-E exists, Eve watches every human super admin and reports to a second human in IT security who is never the subject. And here is the sentence we insist on: inside the tenant's organisation Eve's independence is *detective*, because a super admin can reach anything inside it; what is *structural* is what leaves — the evidence, the incident record and the pager go to a witness organisation, a second Google organisation run by IT security, which sees Eve's deletion within fifteen minutes and does not see a forged approval.
>
> *Mo* is the improver: committed SQL, one deterministic job, optionally a model that is never load-bearing. Every number it publishes is re-derived by a validator Mo cannot reach. It holds no credential, no secret, no signer; it can only propose, and it reaches production through a pull request two humans review, one outside the owner line. Eve reads nothing Mo writes; Eve can lower, Mo can only propose.
>
> **The regulation, in one minute.** The design promises mechanisms and evidence, and lists what the regulator, the guidelines or the assessor still decide. Under the EU AI Act, the duties on literacy, prohibited practices and transparency bind now; the high-risk obligations of Annex III apply from 2027-12-02; Wall-E is classified next to, not inside, the high-risk list, with the assessment signed by legal and registration due before the first write; Eve's control path is not an AI system, held as an invariant a build check enforces. Under TISAX, the automotive industry's security assessment, a robot with Super Admin is a signed deviation from least privilege, and an assessor may refuse the target maturity on that control however good the compensation. Every regulatory date was re-verified on 2026-09-18 and holds.
>
> **What exists and what it costs, in one minute.** Nothing is built. Fourteen design pages, a brief of about two hundred pages, three build procedures and a register of 204 decisions exist; no folder, project, robot or log copy does. On 2026-09-13 the organisation has one administrator, no second super admin outside that administrator's line, no security reviewer, no controller owner and no data protection officer engaged; every role is one person, which is the finding most likely to stop a TISAX assessment. No price is quoted: the pricing pages have not been read, each cost's driver and payer is named, and the binding cost is human hours. No saving is claimed: it exists only after four weeks of today's toil are measured before the doer's first stage, because no "before" can be measured afterwards.
>
> **The ask, in one minute.** Three ways to start, each growing into the next with nothing torn down: two people for three business days, a demonstration of machinery under control that is not compliance evidence and not a production grant; three people for six to nine weeks to its first stage and week 16 to 20 to its second (26 to 34 weeks with one person), a proof of value on which the doer holds no admin role at all; or the full build, six to seven months to the first read-only stage, not before March 2027, with thirteen appointments and thirteen purchase rows started on day one. Two exits are built in: a dated stop-or-continue review at the end of the doer's first writing stage, and a human approving each action as a legitimate permanent end state. A signature is not a commitment to full autonomy.

## Objections a buyer raises, with grounded answers

Each answer is under 150 words and stops where the linked page starts. For an objection not listed, the four rules below hold and the page to open is the one the nearest objection links to:

- nothing is built, and the answer says so first;
- no price and no saving figure exist, and the answer names what produces them;
- no regulator's or assessor's decision is promised, and the answer names what is decided elsewhere;
- Super Admin is the owner's decision, designed around and never re-argued.

### 1. "You are giving a robot Super Admin."

Yes. That is the owner's decision of 2026-09-13, recorded as P33, and this document designs around it and does not re-argue it ([decision record](../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md)). What the design does is name the losses first, without softening: the Workspace-side gate is lost; a leaked token or an interactive login is a compromise of the whole tenant with a path into the cloud organisation; least privilege becomes a signed deviation; detection becomes the primary control ([what this reverses](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs)). Then it makes thirteen compensations preconditions of the grant, none deferred: three lanes decided by code, two signed lists, two credentials in two services, a second human outside the doer's line, kill switches of which K6 — a human removing the role — survives a token already minted, and a permanent ceiling under which no super-admin-class operation is ever autonomous ([Wall-E on the platform](../01-hld.md#131-wall-e--the-super-admin-singleton-in-tier-p-sa)). The role exists only while its compensations do.

### 2. "Eve is not independent."

Inside the tenant's organisation, correct, and the design says so: a Workspace super admin can grant itself Organization Administrator on the tenant's Google Cloud organisation, so nothing inside it is structurally out of reach, Eve's control path included. There, Eve's independence is detective. What leaves is structural: the evidence, the incident record and the pager are pushed — never pulled — to a witness organisation on a separate, minimal identity tenant whose two administrators are from IT security and outside the doer's line; the witness sees Eve's deletion within fifteen minutes and does not see a forged approval ([Eve's independence](../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness)). Moving the control path itself into the witness is the proposed end state, P15, gating the closed tier, not the first grant ([later](../12-open-decisions.md#6-later)). The proof of value and the three-day build have no witness at all, which is why both forbid the sentence "Eve is independent".

### 3. "This is not compliant, and 'EU AI Act bulletproof' is a promise nobody can make."

On the first half: nothing is built, so nothing is compliant or non-compliant yet. From the first day anything exists, literacy, prohibited practices and transparency bind; transparency by construction — the action service, never the model, prepends the disclosure to every message ([Art. 50](../10-eu-ai-act.md#47-art-50--disclosure-on-outbound-messages-on-the-surface-and-the-marking-position-p128)) — and literacy by a recorded briefing per role ([training records](../11-tisax.md#7-separation-of-duties-and-the-staffing-minimum-per-stage-p137)). On the second half, agreed: it promises mechanisms and evidence, and lists what the regulator or the guidelines decide ([what "bulletproof" can and cannot mean](../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean)). Re-verified on 2026-09-18: Annex III obligations apply from 2027-12-02; the classification guidelines are draft; no harmonised standard is cited, so no conformity presumption ([the regulatory state](../10-eu-ai-act.md#1-the-regulatory-state-on-2026-09-13)). The residual list names eleven things it cannot promise, the Super Admin grant first; the mechanisms are real: classification as a gate a build check enforces, legal's signed assessment before the first write, Eve's control path not an AI system ([classification per system](../10-eu-ai-act.md#3-classification-per-system)).

### 4. "What does it cost?"

No price is quoted in the design, because the pricing pages were not read; every amount is *tbd*, and the design names each cost's driver and who pays it ([cost classes](../01-hld.md#05-cost-classes)). Google publishes a subscription floor for Security Command Center Premium, recorded on 2026-09-15 and to be re-verified; it is a vendor's list term, not a price for this platform ([costs on no Google Cloud budget](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#5-costs-that-sit-on-no-google-cloud-budget)). The cost lines are: that subscription; central log ingestion and locked retention, the largest line; the security monitoring service and its round-the-clock retainer; a Workspace licence per privileged agent; Model Armor by request volume; the witness organisation; Chrome Enterprise Premium licences; and human hours, which are the binding cost ([what is not yet priced](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#what-is-not-yet-priced)). Thirteen purchase rows sit on no cloud budget (same page). The benefit side is as honest: no figure exists until four weeks of toil are measured ([why build it](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)).

### 5. "Why not a narrow admin role instead of Super Admin?"

Because the objective of 2026-09-13 asks that the doer perform any super-admin-level Workspace action ([the objective](../00-objective-review.md#1-the-objective)), and no scoped role can hold that: a service account can hold any Workspace admin role except Super Admin, and Super Admin cannot be scoped ([what this reverses](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs); recorded 2026-09-13, to be re-verified). The design declines the literal "any action" — console-only work becomes instructions to a human and the hard-denied list is refused in every lane ([traceability](../01-hld.md#06-traceability-the-objectives-sixteen-requirements-and-the-register-ids-not-cited-elsewhere)) — and names what the role costs before any compensation. That is the owner's decision, P33, designed around here. The two smaller paths run on the narrow roles the objection describes: the proof of value's doer holds no Workspace admin role, and Super Admin goes to no agent ([the two tracks](../pov/README.md#3-the-two-tracks)); the three-day doer holds one organisational-unit-scoped privilege over four synthetic accounts ([the four projects](../3-day/README.md#5-the-four-projects-and-why-the-doers-project-is-not-called-walle)). Neither produces evidence about a super-admin robot, nor claims to.

### 6. "What if Google changes something?"

The design assumes it will, and records every Google fact with the date it was read and the words "to be re-verified" ([sources](../11-tisax.md#16-sources-consulted-on-2026-09-13)). Three facts the safety case depends on are named and kept out of it until a test answers them: whether the principal boundary blocks Cloud Run invocation, whether the Admin console access level applies to super admins, and the deny policy's spelling for agent principals ([what the platform does not do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do)). Every model is pinned against a committed allow-list, and a change of pin, a modification of a model, a final guideline or a cited standard is a reclassification trigger that suspends the register row until re-signed ([reclassification triggers](../10-eu-ai-act.md#316-reclassification-triggers-p132)). Google's products and models enter through one supplier procedure, with an annual review and an exit that exports before it deletes ([supplier onboarding and exit](../11-tisax.md#8-supplier-onboarding-and-exit--third-party-agents-mcp-servers-marketplace-apps-models-p138)).

### 7. "We have no security operations centre."

The platform does not run one. It buys acknowledgement for the privileged tier — a security monitoring service in the EU, a managed detection and response retainer with round-the-clock acknowledgement, a paging service — and runs Google's own detections for the rest; if the organisation has a centre, the platform feeds it ([what the platform does not do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do)). Below the privileged tier the detection desk is stated as "next business morning" ([traceability](../01-hld.md#06-traceability-the-objectives-sixteen-requirements-and-the-register-ids-not-cited-elsewhere)). The design records no centre on 2026-09-13 ([who exists](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs)); `Assumption:` whether one exists elsewhere in the organisation is unknown. The consequence is a gate, not a refusal: the read-only tiers open with the people who exist; the privileged tier and the super-admin grant wait on the purchase, which has the longest lead time of the thirteen and is started on day one ([the purchase table](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#1-the-purchase-table-longest-lead-time-first)).

### 8. "The works council will refuse."

Their answer is a gate the design waits on. Worker information — or consultation, where national law requires it — precedes the first write of any agent acting on employee accounts and is a precondition of the super-admin grant ([workers and the explanation path](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129)); [the cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#54-employee-data-and-the-works-council) asks that it come earlier still, before the controller's first stream about human administrators, because Eve over the administrators is employee monitoring and needs the data protection officer's record first ([P154](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191)). `Assumption:` the jurisdiction is unknown, so whether the council is informed or consulted is HR's answer. The council is shown the purpose, the caps, what Wall-E never does, what is logged, and an explanation path under which no decision is solely automated ([workers](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#227-workers-information-consultation-and-the-explanation-path-p129)). If the answer is no, no agent writes to an employee account and Eve does not stream about administrators until it changes; the design waits.

### 9. "It is not built."

Correct, and every document says so first. What exists is documentation: fourteen design pages, a brief of about two hundred pages, a full build of README plus forty-two files with twenty-two rows BLOCKED on code, a contract and people that do not exist, a proof of value of README plus nine files, and a three-day build whose seven code files are the only code in the wiki ([BLOCKED index](../setup/README.md#8-blocked-index); [what exists](../README.md#maturity-what-exists-on-2026-09-13)). The paths are ordered so nothing is torn down; a three-day build that has to be torn down has failed ([how it grows](../3-day/README.md#12-how-it-grows)). [The cross-check review of 2026-09-18](../14-crosscheck-review.md#4-security) read the three-day code as an attacker would: the twenty-eight corrections the day files owed were made that day ([corrections made](../3-day/README.md#61-corrections-made-on-2026-09-18)), and one defect still stands, the halt lever writable by the process it halts, to be removed before the run; none is a flaw in the stance.

### 10. "Who is liable when it goes wrong?"

Under the EU AI Act the organisation is both provider and deployer of Wall-E and Mo; Google provides the models and the Gemini Enterprise application; the legal entity is P23, *tbd* (`Assumption:` the employing entity) ([roles](../10-eu-ai-act.md#2-roles--which-legal-entity-is-provider-and-which-is-deployer)). An AI compliance owner, legal's designate and never the platform owner, is accountable for classifications, assessments, registrations and serious-incident reports ([P131](../10-eu-ai-act.md#41-the-ai-compliance-owner-p131)). The residual risk of a super-admin robot is signed by the security reviewer, accepted by the platform owner and entered by the ISMS ([P140](../11-tisax.md#10-the-risk-register-p140)). For an employee harmed by an action, the organisation is the data controller of its own tenant and the frame is its existing GDPR programme ([the position](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#221-the-position-controller-and-internal-processing)); the stated residual is that a wrongful suspension can last the hold window plus the restore's human approval, up to four business hours, and the explanation path runs through HR ([bounded](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#224-the-actions-that-affect-employees-and-how-they-are-bounded)). No decision on the platform is solely automated.

### 11. "What about AGI?"

Declined for 2026. Agents of general capability — capabilities beyond the designers' foresight, code execution, self-directed goals — belong to Tier X, a folder that is not open ([the honest line](../01-hld.md#115-the-honest-line)). Of its opening conditions, one is met: the sandbox tier exists in the EU at general availability. The others — a provider capability-evaluation report per model pin, an AI-safety reviewer role, the fleet kill switch drilled for six months, an independent-model advisory monitor running, every buildable containment row live — are not in reach in 2026 ([the tier gate](../01-hld.md#04-the-tier-gate-what-must-exist-before-a-tier-opens)). The platform does not evaluate model capabilities, detect deception or claim AGI containment ([what the platform does not do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do)). What it does give such an agent, when the day comes, is the same six containment primitives every agent gets, and a fleet kill switch that lives outside every agent project.

### 12. "Why three agents? One would be simpler."

Because the design forbids one program from both deciding and acting, and from grading its own work ([the platform charter](../01-hld.md#1-the-platform-charter)). The doer acts and holds the credential in its action service. The controller decides — approves, halts, demotes — and its decision path contains no model, holds no Workspace write and reads no doer secret. The improver measures both and can only propose; it holds no credential, is never the controller's grader, and reaches production only through a pull request two humans merge ([Mo's thesis](../../mo/01-hld.md#thesis)). Collapse any two and a property is lost: a doer that grades itself, a controller that can be argued with, an improver that can push. Eve can lower, Mo can only propose, humans raise. The three are also the platform's first tenants, not its definition: the hundredth agent costs a register row and a factory run ([thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis)).

### 13. "The model will be manipulated — prompt injection is unsolved."

Yes, and the design says manipulated content will reach the model; it is the fifth residual risk a signature accepts ([the residual risks](../brief/23-threat-model-and-residual-risk.md#the-residual-risks-a-sponsor-accepts-by-signing)). A probabilistic content screen — Model Armor, a model acting as judge — is never a trust boundary whatever its grade; it produces evidence ([the six containment primitives](../01-hld.md#02-the-six-containment-primitives-and-how-each-is-graded)). The enforcement sits outside the model's process: the model holds no credential; the action service refuses anything outside the catalogue, on the hard-denied list, off the target's organisational unit or above the level cap; the first level forces a dry run; execution needs a second human; the write-ahead audit row exists before any call. A manipulated model can propose a bad plan; it cannot execute one that the ceilings refuse, and the reconciled count of robot events with no matching audit row has a target of zero ([Mo on the platform](../01-hld.md#133-mo--improving-both-one-mo-per-platform-without-touching-eves-independence)).

### 14. "Why six to seven months? We were told three weeks."

"A sponsor told 'three weeks' has been misled about both stages" is the proof of value's own sentence ([the two stages](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages)). The calendar is set by lead times and stage floors, not by engineering: the security monitoring contract takes months (`Assumption:`), the penetration test weeks to months, a new security key up to seven days to work at sign-in (`Assumption:`); the doer's stages have floors of three to twelve weeks each, and the super-admin grant waits on five named humans ([parallel sittings and the critical path](../setup/README.md#34-parallel-sittings-and-the-critical-path)). What three weeks buys, honestly, is less than the three-day build claims: nothing about super admin, no witness, no baseline. The three-day build takes three days because it cuts twenty-seven things, each safe only because the population is synthetic, the run is attended and nothing is claimed as compliance evidence ([the cut list](../3-day/README.md#8-the-cut-list-c-01-to-c-27)).
