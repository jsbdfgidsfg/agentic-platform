# Questions people ask

## Status
- Owner: the platform owner
- Last reviewed: 2026-09-18
- What this page is: thirty-nine questions people actually ask about the platform and its three agents, grouped by who asks them, each answered in plain words with a link to the page that holds the precise version.

## In one sentence

Nothing is built, no price is quoted, no regulator's decision is promised, and the honest answer to most of these questions is "the design says this, and here is what it still cannot claim".

## How to read this page

Find your own heading and read down. Every answer stands on its own and is under 120 words. An **agent** is a program that uses a language model (software that writes text and plans from text) to decide what to do. It may be given tools to act. **Wall-E** is the doer, **Eve** the controller, **Mo** the improver ([02-the-three-agents.md](02-the-three-agents.md)). The platform sits on the organisation's Google Cloud (its rented computing) and Google Workspace (its mail, calendar, documents and user accounts). Where a date is marked `Assumption:` it is an earliest date, not a plan.

## An employee asks

**Will it read my email?**
Not the text of it. Wall-E is an administration assistant. It reads directory, licence, group and audit data: who has an account, which licence and groups it has, and Google's record of administrative changes. The only mailbox it touches is its own, where it sets labels. It writes to employees only through templated notices that say they come from an AI system. Eve watches administrators through activity records (who signed in, who changed what), never message content. One store does copy every account's sign-in events for 400 days (`Assumption:` until the data protection officer confirms). Nothing is built yet ([the declared purpose](../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on); [where a person is identifiable](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#223-where-a-person-is-identifiable-along-one-request)).

**Can it delete my account?**
No. Deleting data is on the hard-denied list, the list of operations the design refuses in every lane (each of its three ways of acting), even when a human asks. What it can do is suspend an account, and only after the organisation's HR (human resources) process or a named operator (a human allowed to instruct it) has already decided that; a request on a person's account must cite that decision record or it is refused. Restoring an account always needs a human approval. The residual is stated plainly: a wrongful suspension can last up to four business hours before the restore is approved ([what Wall-E never does](../10-eu-ai-act.md#311-the-declared-intended-purpose-the-sentence-the-assessment-rests-on); [the actions that affect employees](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#224-the-actions-that-affect-employees-and-how-they-are-bounded)).

**Can it decide that I am leaving, or judge my work?**
No. The declared purpose says it never decides who leaves, who is promoted, who is monitored or how anyone performs, and never selects persons by their behaviour. A report of accounts unused for 90 days is a document for a human to read. A licence can be reclaimed from an active account only from a list a human supplied, and both the build checks and the running code refuse targets taken from a report. If legal rules that even this is profiling (judging people from patterns in their data), the design's recommendation is to remove that operation rather than widen the robot ([the profiling boundary](../10-eu-ai-act.md#314-the-profiling-question-answered-by-design-p126)).

**Will I be told when it acts on my account?**
Two ways. Every message it sends carries a fixed line saying it comes from an AI system. The action service (the code that holds the credential, the robot's key to Google, never the model) adds that line, so it cannot be left out; a message that cannot carry it is refused. Before the first write on any real account, two documents are published. The data protection officer (the person legally responsible for how personal data is handled) publishes an employee notice naming Wall-E as an AI system acting on accounts. HR publishes an information pack. That first write does not happen without the date of both ([the disclosure rule, P128](../12-open-decisions.md#5-before-wall-es-stage-1-and-its-later-stages); [the information pack](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129)).

**Can I find out why something happened to my account?**
Yes, through HR. No decision on the platform is made by the machine alone. Every action keeps its frozen plan, its reasons, the state before, what triggered it and which human decision it cites. HR obtains that, removes other people's data and the raw prompt, and answers within a number of business days not yet set. Every request is logged and counted, and one unanswered past the deadline is a recorded finding ([the explanation path](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129)).

**Is anything happening to my account today?**
No. On 2026-09-18 nothing is built: no robot account, no watcher, no register (the file every agent must be in before it exists). The first thing that could exist is a three-day demonstration, planned for 2026-09-22 to 2026-09-24 (`Assumption:`), on four made-up accounts; its procedure refuses to run if a real account is in its test group of accounts. A real account enters any pilot only on three conditions. A completed and signed data protection impact assessment, a written study of the risks to people before processing starts. HR's written answer on the works council. And the holder's written consent ([the pilot population, P197](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204); [the nine absolutes](../3-day/README.md#2-the-nine-absolutes)).

## A manager asks

**What would it actually do for my team?**
From its read-only stage: weekly digests of last week's changes, accounts unused for 90 days, suspended accounts still licensed, groups with outside members or no owner, and administrators checked against a signed list. From its first writing stage: leaver and joiner actions from a chat request, and licences reclaimed from suspended accounts. Later, one operator approving a whole planned batch. No benefit figure exists, and none may be quoted until four weeks of today's manual work have been measured first, because no "before" can be measured afterwards ([why build it](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)).

**Who tells it what to do?**
A named operator, in a chat; later, a catalogued trigger: a schedule, an event from the HR system, or an item in its own inbox. Every operation sits on a ladder of six levels, from L0 (off) to L5 (autonomous with independent checks). Humans raise a level, on evidence; machines can only lower one. At the first stage every writing operation runs at L1, a forced dry run: it records what it would have done and changes nothing. That it will one day act without a fresh prompt is a reading the owner has still to confirm in writing ([the ladder](../../wall-e/05-autonomy-ladder.md#stage-overview); [the reading awaiting the owner](../brief/30-decisions-awaiting-owner.md#every-unresolved-point-the-brief-raises)).

**If I approve a run, what am I responsible for?**
The decision, not the typing. An operator never approves what they themselves requested; the code refuses it. Uncatalogued super-admin work needs one human super admin to specify it fully and a second to approve. Approvals are sampled: a blind grader, a named person who did not write the routines, checks a weekly sample without knowing which items were planted. A fall below the threshold opens a review item, never an automatic consequence for a person. Approvers are also measured for automation bias, waving things through, which the design measures and does not claim to eliminate ([the roles](../11-tisax.md#71-the-roles); [operators as data subjects](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#226-operators-as-data-subjects-the-limits-on-monitoring)).

**What happens if it goes wrong?**
Anyone may halt its writes with one call, no approval needed; reads continue. Each operation family has its own rollback, and the design says plainly that it does not back up Workspace: recovery beyond that is Google's own. A wrongful suspension is undone by a human-approved restore. Serious incidents follow the reporting clock of the EU AI Act (the European Union's law on artificial intelligence), 15, 10 or 2 days, voluntarily. Personal-data breaches follow the path of the GDPR (the EU's General Data Protection Regulation), with the data protection officer accountable. Nothing has been drilled, because nothing is built ([the kill switches](../../wall-e/ARCHITECTURE.md#46-kill-switches); [what the platform does not do](../01-hld.md#16-what-the-platform-does-not-do); [incident deadlines, P101](../12-open-decisions.md#4-before-the-super-admin-grant)).

**Can it act without anyone asking?**
Only for catalogued work that has climbed the ladder on evidence, and never for operations that need the full administrator power (Super Admin, the Workspace role that can do everything), which stay permanently at L3, a human approving each one. Suspending an account can reach L4 only on the HR system's own event, held for a veto in a window that never runs out outside business hours; from chat or a schedule it stays at L3. Restoring an account stays at L3 for ever ([the level caps, P127](../10-eu-ai-act.md#312-annex-iii-4b-family-by-family); [the thirteen compensations](../01-hld.md#131-wall-e--the-super-admin-singleton-in-tier-p-sa)).

**When does it start?**
Every date is an earliest date and an `Assumption:`. The three-day demonstration is planned for 2026-09-22 to 2026-09-24. A proof of value starting 2026-09-21 would end its first stage between 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-27, and its second between 2027-01-15 and 2027-02-12 with a dedicated engineer. The full build reaches its first stage, reads only, 6 to 7 months after it starts and not before 2027-03; the first write on a real account comes after that. The proof-of-value page says it: "A sponsor told 'three weeks' has been misled about both stages." ([the two stages](../pov/README.md#4-pov_stage_dates-the-two-stages); [the critical path](../setup/README.md#34-parallel-sittings-and-the-critical-path)).

## A works-council member asks

**Is this employee monitoring?**
Yes, in two ways. Wall-E reads every employee's directory, licence, group and audit data from its first stage and later acts on accounts. Eve watches every human administrator from its first run, before Wall-E exists: every super admin and admin-role holder, the platform owner included. It records their administrative actions, sign-ins and tokens (the passes programs use to act), not content, kept 400 days with a copy outside the organisation. GDPR Article 88 names monitoring systems at the workplace, and a data protection officer's record must exist before Eve's first feed ([the monitoring record, P154](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191); [what the platform owes the DPO](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#222-what-the-platform-owes-the-dpo-and-where-each-input-stands)).

**When are we told, and is it information or consultation?**
The country, and so the exact duty, is not fixed. The design puts your information, or consultation where the law requires it, before the first write on employee accounts and before the robot's account is given full administrator power (the super-admin grant). Eve's watching of administrators starts earlier than both. The review of 2026-09-18 asks for the trigger to move to Eve's first stream, with consultation as the default and consent where co-determination (the council's legal right to agree first) applies. The register row P129 still says Wall-E's first writing stage (Stage 1), unchanged so far. A council told later would have been bypassed for two stages ([workers and the explanation path, P129](../10-eu-ai-act.md#410-art-267-2611-art-86--workers-and-the-explanation-path-p129); [the legal register, P141](../11-tisax.md#11-the-legal-and-contractual-register-p141)).

**Even the three-day demonstration?**
Yes. Its watcher reads the organisation's real administrator audit log (Google's record of every administrative action) from its first hour, which is monitoring of real employees whatever accounts the doer acts on. On 2026-09-18 the position is this. The data protection officer's record and HR's written answer must exist before day one. Failing that, the watching is limited to the two participants, each with written consent ([what must be true before day 1](../3-day/README.md#6-what-must-be-true-before-day-1-starts); [the DPO record, P199](../12-open-decisions.md#6b-proposed-by-the-proof-of-value-set-p192p204)).

**Can a machine suspend someone on its own?**
Only in one case. On an event from the HR system of record, where HR has already decided, the suspension may run at L4: it waits in a hold a human can veto, and the hold never expires outside business hours. Any suspension asked for in chat or by a schedule needs a human approval. Restoring always does. A write on a person's account through band B, the lane for requests outside the fixed list, must cite the decision record or is refused. The residual: a wrongful suspension can last the hold plus the approval, up to four business hours ([the actions that affect employees](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#224-the-actions-that-affect-employees-and-how-they-are-bounded); [the residual risk statement](../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement)).

**Does it judge people by their behaviour?**
No output of the model selects a person by behaviour. The operation that reclaims licences is split in two: from suspended accounts, and from inactive ones. The inactivity report is a document for a human; targets for reclaim from active accounts come only from ids a human supplied, and the code refuses targets taken from a report. The design names the risk of inactivity rules falling on part-time, sick, disabled and on-leave staff. Whether inactivity reclaim counts as profiling is a question for legal and the data protection officer (P18, open) ([the profiling boundary](../10-eu-ai-act.md#314-the-profiling-question-answered-by-design-p126); [the fundamental-rights column](../10-eu-ai-act.md#42-art-9--the-fundamental-rights-column-and-the-residual-risk-statement)).

**What will we be shown?**
An evidence register lists fifteen artefacts, who produces each and how long it is kept; which rows the council receives is decided with the council. Closest to you: the information pack and employee notice, the explanation log, the fundamental-rights risk rows, the roster of who oversees, and the record permitting the monitoring of administrators. Employee representatives join the monitoring part of the crisis rehearsal and receive the post-incident review where national law requires it (`Assumption:`) ([the evidence register](../10-eu-ai-act.md#5-evidence-register); [what the works council is shown](../brief/26-personal-data-and-employees.md#227-workers-information-consultation-and-the-explanation-path-p129)).

## A security person asks

**Why give a robot Super Admin at all?**
Because the platform owner decided it on 2026-09-13 (decision P33), and the design works around it rather than re-arguing it. Super Admin is the Workspace role with every administrative power. Two Google facts follow. A service account (the identity a program normally uses) cannot hold Super Admin, so the robot is a user account with a licence and two hardware keys (physical sign-in devices). And Super Admin cannot be limited to part of the organisation or a subset of its powers. The design names what that loses: the Workspace-side gate, a bounded blast radius, Eve's structural independence, least privilege as a property. Detection becomes the primary control ([the decision record](../../../decisions/2026-09-13-wall-e-holds-super-admin.md); [what this reverses and what it costs](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs)).

**Who watches the watcher?**
Four things. Mo measures Eve's detection quality, but Eve reads nothing Mo writes and Mo can only propose. Eve carries thirteen rules about its own integrity. A witness organisation, a second Google organisation run by IT security, sits outside the reach of the tenant's super admins (the tenant being the organisation's whole Workspace) and holds a copy of Eve's evidence. It sees the deletion of Eve's project within fifteen minutes, and the absence of evidence. It does not see a forged approval. And a second human, in IT security and outside the Wall-E line, owns Eve. Who receives reports about that second human is still unfilled ([Mo's thesis](../../mo/01-hld.md#thesis); [Eve-H, P153](../12-open-decisions.md#6a-proposed-by-the-setup-procedures-p144p191); [what the witness sees](../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness); [the blocked index](../setup/README.md#8-blocked-index)).

**Can we switch it off, and how fast?**
Eight switches, none yet drilled. K0 halts writes: anyone, no approval, under 5 seconds at the endpoint, containment under 60. K4 revokes the credential in seconds, but an access token (a short-lived pass already issued) can work for up to 60 minutes (`Assumption:`). K5 suspends the robot account: a human, within 30 minutes of the most serious alarm (severity 1) being acknowledged. K6 removes Super Admin, human only, within 60 minutes: the one switch that survives a token already minted. Whether Google's multi-party approval (a setting needing a second admin for covered changes) makes it two-person is unresolved. K7 stops every agent, under 5 minutes, never by a model ([the kill switches](../../wall-e/ARCHITECTURE.md#46-kill-switches); [the fleet kill switch](../01-hld.md#114-the-fleet-kill-switch-k7-and-the-containment-primitives-for-the-top-tier)).

**What happens if the credential is stolen?**
The design says it without softening. A leaked token or an interactive login is a takeover of the whole Workspace tenant. It also opens a path into the Google Cloud estate, Eve's and Mo's projects included, because a super admin can grant itself the top cloud role. Custody of hardware keys, a split of the credential across two services, detection within minutes and people pulling switches meet it; nothing prevents it. It is risk row one, accepted by the security reviewer's signature, and that role is not yet filled ([the residual risks a sponsor accepts](../brief/23-threat-model-and-residual-risk.md#the-residual-risks-a-sponsor-accepts-by-signing); [the risk register, P140](../11-tisax.md#10-the-risk-register-p140)).

**Is Eve really independent?**
Inside the organisation, no: its independence there is detective, not structural, because a super admin can reach anything inside, Eve included. What leaves the organisation is structural: the evidence, the incident record and the pager go to the witness organisation, which is pushed to and never pulls. The end state (P15, proposed) moves Eve's control path there too, once IT security operates it. That move is a condition for opening the top tier, the closed one reserved for agents of general capability. It is not a condition of the super-admin grant. The two smaller build paths have no witness at all and forbid the sentence "Eve is independent" ([Eve's independence, stated honestly](../../eve/01-hld.md#1-eves-independence-detective-inside-the-organisation-structural-through-the-witness); [the end state, P15](../12-open-decisions.md#6-later)).

**Can the language model approve anything?**
No. The model holds no credential and cannot approve; no model produces an Eve approval, signature, halt, veto or refusal. Eve's control path keeps the model out by absence: the model service is denied at project level and a build check refuses any model client. Eve's reporting path may reason, but writes only narratives and advice that nothing on the decision path reads. Model Armor, Google's screen for text going into and out of a model, works by probability, so it produces evidence and is never a trust boundary ([the standing constraints](../01-hld.md#status); [Eve's two paths](../01-hld.md#132-eve--independent-controller-two-paths-a-witness-outside-the-tenant-a-second-human); [how each primitive is graded](../01-hld.md#02-the-six-containment-primitives-and-how-each-is-graded)).

**What is enforced and what is only detected?**
Every control is one or the other; nothing only detected stands alone in a safety argument. Enforced: the permissions granted to the robot's application, never including the one that opens all of Google Cloud. No domain-wide delegation (a mechanism letting a program act as any user). The hard-denied list, the forced dry run and the write-ahead audit in the action service. The model kept out of Eve's control path. Google's multi-party approval on role assignment. Detected only: any use of the robot's credential outside its two services. Any act of a super admin against Eve, the log feeds or the evidence inside the organisation. For the super-admin tier, detection is the primary control ([the six containment primitives](../01-hld.md#02-the-six-containment-primitives-and-how-each-is-graded); [what this reverses](../01-hld.md#what-this-reverses-and-what-it-costs)).

**Would a security architect sign the deviation today?**
No, and the security verdict of 2026-09-18 says why. The deviation record is the signed file admitting that the robot's Super Admin breaks the least-privilege control of TISAX (the automotive industry's security assessment) and listing the compensations under which the exception is accepted. Three of its gate rows are not green: row 5, the bought detection desk with round-the-clock acknowledgement together with the witness organisation; row 8, the perimeter test; and row 11, a second human outside the administration line. Five more rows cannot be proven while the service code does not exist. The signature is the security reviewer's, a role nobody holds ([the checklist the gate reads](../11-tisax.md#63-compensations-as-preconditions--the-checklist-the-gate-reads); [who exists on 2026-09-13](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs)).

## A sponsor or board member asks

**What does it cost?**
No price is quoted anywhere; pricing pages were not read and every amount is to be decided. The design names each cost's driver and payer. The lines are Google's organisation-wide security findings subscription, log storage (the largest line), a licence per privileged agent, the witness organisation, browser licences, and human hours, the binding cost. One more is a security information and event management service, the system that collects logs, runs detections and staffs a desk that answers alarms, with a round-the-clock retainer. The one recorded figure is a subscription minimum of 15,000 USD a year for 12 months, as recorded on 2026-09-15, to be re-verified ([cost classes](../01-hld.md#05-cost-classes); [costs on no cloud budget](../setup/04-purchases-and-lead-times.md#5-costs-that-sit-on-no-google-cloud-budget)).

**What do we get?**
No benefit figure exists yet, and none can until four weeks of today's manual work on the top three administrative tasks are measured before the doer's first stage; no "before" can be measured afterwards. The gain claimed without a number: routine administration done faster and on the record, weekly digests, leaver and joiner actions from chat, licence reclaim, and batch approval. Also a hundredth agent costing a register row and a factory run. The register is the file every agent must be in before it exists; the factory is the pipeline that builds its secured project ([why build it](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop); [the thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis)).

**Is this legal?**
Nobody can promise a regulator's decision. What holds on 2026-09-18, re-verified: the EU AI Act's literacy, prohibition and transparency duties bind now. Its high-risk obligations bind from 2027-12-02. Wall-E is classed next to, not inside, the high-risk list, against guidelines still in draft. It must be registered in the EU database before its first write. GDPR applies always, with the organisation as controller (the party answerable for the data). TISAX, the automotive industry's security assessment, is a site's label. An assessor may refuse the required maturity for a super-admin robot. The design promises mechanisms and evidence and lists what the regulator still decides ([the position in one paragraph](../10-eu-ai-act.md#0-the-position-in-one-paragraph); [Wall-E's class](../10-eu-ai-act.md#31-wall-e-wall-e); [TISAX in one paragraph](../11-tisax.md#0-what-this-page-decides-in-one-paragraph)).

**What does "EU AI Act bulletproof" mean, then?**
It was declined as a promise. The design contains most of the engineering a high-risk programme needs. An audit kept 400 days with a copy outside the tenant. A model-free verifier with halt authority. A ladder whose every move is a pre-declared change. A gate that makes classification a precondition of existence. What it cannot say: the narrow-task claim is made against draft guidelines. No official European standard exists yet to measure it against. The Super Admin grant is the fact most likely held against it. Penalties are real now. The phrase used instead: "it promises the mechanisms and the evidence, and lists what the regulator or the guidelines still decide" ([what "bulletproof" can and cannot mean](../10-eu-ai-act.md#6-what-bulletproof-can-and-cannot-mean)).

**What would make you stop the programme?**
Two exits are written down; three more things stop it in practice. First, at the end of the first writing stage a dated review sets work saved against operating cost, human hours included, and stops the programme if it is negative. Second, a human approving every action is a legitimate permanent end state. The other three: a proof of value ends at a stop-or-continue review, and the three-day build at its same-day unwind. After the grant, two gate rows red at once, or the detection, hygiene or second-human row red at all, removes Super Admin until green. Funding the build but not the people gives a read-only platform ([how the programme can stop](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop); [the red-row rule](../11-tisax.md#63-compensations-as-preconditions--the-checklist-the-gate-reads); [the tier gate](../brief/29-roadmap-and-cost.md#the-tier-gate)).

**What am I signing?**
Eight residual risks, in order of consequence. A stolen robot credential is a tenant takeover. Inside the organisation the controller watches a super admin but cannot structurally stop one. The design's safety depends on people who do not exist yet. The robot's gate is one process its build pipeline can rewrite. Manipulated content will reach the model. The regulator and the assessor decide outcomes. Some Google facts are unverified and kept out of the safety case. And there are accepted operational limits. In the design's words, "a signature is not a commitment to full autonomy" ([the residual risks a sponsor accepts](../brief/23-threat-model-and-residual-risk.md#the-residual-risks-a-sponsor-accepts-by-signing); [why build it](../brief/02-executive-summary.md#why-build-it-and-how-the-programme-can-stop)).

**Who has to exist before it can run?**
On 2026-09-13: one administrator, no second super admin outside that administrator's line, no security reviewer, no Eve owner, no engaged data protection officer, no detection desk. Read-only tiers need nobody new. Writing needs a second operator, a security reviewer and a blind grader: three distinct humans. The super-admin grant needs four, five in the clean state, and a bought round-the-clock desk at the third stage. Every role on the chart being one person is the finding most likely to stop a TISAX assessment ([who exists and the roles needed](../01-hld.md#03-who-exists-on-2026-09-13-and-the-roles-the-platform-needs); [the minimum per stage](../11-tisax.md#72-the-minimum-per-stage)).

**Can we really add hundreds of agents?**
"A read-only assistant costs a register row and a factory run. A super-admin robot costs a witness organisation, a second super admin, a bought detection desk and a signed deviation." The cap is not projects but blind-grading capacity. Every writing agent needs about an hour a week of a human grading its work, and that ceiling is an open decision (P25). Agents of general capability, the top tier, are closed. Of the conditions to open it, none beyond the first is in reach in 2026 ([the thesis](../01-hld.md#01-thesis); [the tier model](../01-hld.md#11-the-tier-model); [the honest line on the top tier](../01-hld.md#115-the-honest-line)).

## An engineer asks

**What is built today?**
Nothing. The full build carries 22 blocked rows: the factory modules, the register checks, the fleet-kill executor, Eve's schemas and reconciler, Mo's jobs, Wall-E's services, one contract and the people. The only code in the wiki is the three-day build's, about 1,350 lines inside fenced blocks (`Assumption:`, measured 2026-09-18). Its twenty-eight owed corrections were all made on 2026-09-18. The security verdict of that day still names two defects in the corrected files: a halt (the stop lever) that the halted process can clear, and three settings that fail open, meaning a missing value loosens a check instead of stopping the program. Every project id is to be decided ([the blocked index](../setup/README.md#8-blocked-index); [the agent-set projects](../../project-topology.md#2-the-four-projects); [the corrections made](../3-day/README.md#61-corrections-made-on-2026-09-18)).

**What can the three-day build not show?**
Two people, three days, four synthetic accounts, no Super Admin to any agent, no witness, no bought detection service, no penetration test (a hired attack to find weaknesses), no factory, no Gemini Enterprise (Google's front-door app through which people reach agents). It is a demonstration of machinery under control, not compliance evidence and not a production grant. That is said to the sponsor before the demonstration. Four sentences are never to be used in any report, slide or message about it. Each is true of the full design and false of what three days produced ([what three days cannot buy](../3-day/README.md#7-what-three-days-cannot-buy); [never to be used](../3-day/README.md#11-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message)):

> 1. "Wall-E is safe as a super admin." Nothing here touches super-admin containment. No agent holds Super Admin at any moment of the three days.
> 2. "Eve is independent." Independence in the design is structural: a witness organisation, a second tenant. This set has neither (C-01), and its Eve lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches.
> 3. "Model Armor blocked the injection." A probabilistic content screen is never a trust boundary. And nothing here exercises it: no step sends anything through the floor, so these three days produce **no Model Armor evidence at all**, only the floor settings themselves (C-22).
> 4. "We saved `<n>` minutes of admin work." The doer acted only on synthetic accounts. No human minute was saved, and Mo's scorecard says so in bold (C-07).

**And the proof of value?**
Three people and one engineer, on the production tenant. Its first stage ends 6 to 9 weeks after day one; its second ends in week 16 to 20 with a dedicated engineer. It builds the no-code, read-only and writing tiers, a doer holding no Workspace admin role at all, and gives Super Admin to no agent, ever. Nothing it produces satisfies a gate of the super-admin grant; a clause whose record is missing is struck, not softened. It forbids three sentences, for its own reasons ([never to be used about the POV](../pov/README.md#12-never-to-be-used-in-any-report-slide-or-message-about-the-pov); [a POV record never satisfies a full-set gate](../pov/README.md#73-a-pov-record-never-satisfies-a-full-set-gate)):

> 1. "Wall-E is safe as a super admin." Nothing in Track A touches super-admin containment (G10, G11, G14, G20, Eve G-7 need Track B, §3). Nor may any report say or imply that the doer "does admin work": it holds no Workspace admin role.
> 2. "Eve is independent." Independence in the design is structural (a witness organisation, a second tenant). The POV has neither (PV-D-03), and its Eve lives inside the reach of the administrators it watches.
> 3. "Model Armor blocked the injection." A probabilistic content screen is never a trust boundary, whatever its grade. It produced evidence.

**Why one project per agent?**
Forced by Google, not chosen: every Agent Runtime agent in one project and region must bind to the same gateway instances, and the cross-project role the front-door app needs is project-wide. A project id is never reusable and a project never moves between tier folders. That is why the proof of value's doer is a separate agent with its own name, and Wall-E's names are reserved for the full build ([why four projects](../../project-topology.md#1-why-four-projects); [the two tracks](../pov/README.md#3-the-two-tracks)).

**Where do I start reading?**
The high-level design is the parent of every design page; read its thesis, its six containment primitives and its tier gate first. The register holds every platform decision, P1 to P204, with its state, and no program answers a row. The design set's front page has a reading order for a security reviewer and one for a builder. Then the build procedure you are asked to run, whose file wins over its README on a command or a name ([the high-level design](../01-hld.md#01-thesis); [the register](../12-open-decisions.md#1-how-this-register-works); [reading order for a builder](../README.md#reading-order-for-a-builder)).

## What this means for you

**If you are an employee.** The two answers that matter most are "not the text of your email" and "it never decides who leaves". Both are design statements about something not yet built. If an action ever does touch your account, you ask HR why, and HR must answer from the record.

**If you are a works-council member.** Eve's watching of administrators starts before Wall-E exists. The regulatory re-verification of 2026-09-18 asks for your information or consultation to be triggered there, at Eve's first stream, with consultation as the default, not at Wall-E's first write. The register row still says Wall-E's first writing stage (P129). The country, and so the exact duty, is not fixed; ask HR which.

**If you are a sponsor.** The three questions to hold onto: no price is quoted, no benefit figure exists, and the people the design depends on do not yet exist. The exits are written down, and the eight residual risks are what your signature accepts.
